## 0. Reduction used throughout (DERIVED)

Notation: alphabet A, map a -> M_a in GL(d,R), c(M) := rank(M - I) = codim Fix(M).
Let H = <M_a : a in A> and pi: free group F(A) -> G the given evaluation.

* The system succeeds iff there is a vector h0 such that M_w h0 = M_w' h0 implies
  pi(w) = pi(w'). This forces (i) ker(F(A) -> H) <= ker(pi), i.e. there is an
  epimorphism H -> G with M_a |-> a, and (ii) Stab_H(h0) <= ker(H -> G).
* If H is finite, or countable, a generic h0 has trivial stabilizer (the fixed spaces
  of nontrivial elements are countably many proper subspaces). So (ii) is free and the
  problem is purely: **k_min(G,A) = min over linear groups H generated by elements of
  cost <= k that admit an epimorphism H -> G sending generator a to a.** Dimension d is
  free. This is exactly question (3) built in: H is always allowed to be bigger than G.
* A finite H is conjugate to a subgroup of O(d) (averaged inner product), and conjugation
  preserves c. So in the finite-image regime one may assume orthogonality for free.

### The decisive fork: is the image H required to be finite (or compact and discrete)?

* **Unrestricted image (infinite H allowed): the problem collapses.**
  - Non-orthogonal: Sanov's matrices [[1,2],[0,1]], [[1,0],[2,1]] are transvections
    (c = 1) and generate a free group of rank 2 (seen in search results; primary source
    UNVERIFIED, I. N. Sanov 1947). Ping-pong with m parabolic transvections with distinct
    fixed points gives F_m (DERIVED, standard Schottky argument). Since every G generated
    by A is a quotient of F(A): **k_min = 1 for every finite group and every alphabet,
    d = 2.** The price is unbounded state norm.
  - Orthogonal (norm-preserving) but infinite allowed: c = 1 elements are reflections
    (involutions), so k = 1 forces every letter to be an involution or the identity in G.
    Conversely, if all letters are involutions, generic orthogonal reflections generate
    the universal Coxeter group Z/2 * ... * Z/2 (DERIVED: Tits' geometric representation
    of the universal Coxeter group is faithful; its form 2I - J is nondegenerate for
    m >= 3, so the tuple lives in O(m, C); the reflection-tuple variety is irreducible and
    real orthogonal tuples are Zariski dense, so each relation fails on a dense open set;
    for m = 2 use an irrational angle). For general letters, two generic rotations of
    SO(3) (c = 2) generate a free group (Epstein 1971, see Domain 2). So
    **k_min in {1, 2}: k = 1 iff all letters are involutions or trivial in G, else k = 2.**
* **Finite image (the interesting regime).** Everything below assumes H finite unless
  stated. Recommendation to the team: state explicitly which regime the abstract problem
  means; finite precision or a requirement that states stay well separated pushes toward
  the finite regime.

## Domain 1 (most promising): finite reflection groups and their codimension-k generalizations

Literature actually seen:
* Shephard, Todd, "Finite unitary reflection groups", Canad. J. Math. 6 (1954) 274-304,
  DOI 10.4153/CJM-1954-028-3.
* Carter, "Conjugacy classes in the Weyl group", Compositio Math. 25 (1972) 1-59
  (Numdam CM_1972__25_1_1_0). Geometric characterisation of reflection length.
* Cartan-Dieudonne-Scherk theorem as stated in search results, including arXiv:1011.1027
  ("An algorithm for the Cartan-Dieudonne theorem on generalized scalar product spaces");
  Scherk's original paper UNVERIFIED. Related: Brady-McCammond, "Factoring euclidean
  isometries", arXiv:1312.7780.
* Lange, Mikhailova, "Classification of finite groups generated by reflections and
  rotations", arXiv:1509.06922 (full text read: Theorem 1 lists the irreducible rotation
  groups).
* Martino, Singh, "Finite groups generated in low real codimension", arXiv:1804.05089
  (they define "generated in codimension k").
* Huffman, "Linear groups containing an element with an eigenspace of codimension two",
  J. Algebra 34 (1975) 260-287; Huffman-Wales, Linear and Multilinear Algebra 3 (1975/76)
  53-59. Both seen only as citations in a search result; content UNVERIFIED.

(a) Shared principle. A finite linear group generated by elements whose fixed space has
codimension <= k, together with an epimorphism onto G, is exactly a solution with cost k;
the cost c(g) = codim Fix(g) equals the minimal number of reflections in a factorisation
of g.

(b) What follows (with exact hypotheses):
1. **Scherk / Cartan-Dieudonne.** Hypotheses: finite-dimensional vector space over a field
   of characteristic != 2 with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form; g an isometry,
   g != id, F = Fix(g), n = dim F^perp. If F^perp is not totally isotropic, g is a product
   of n and not fewer reflections; if it is, n + 2. Over R with a positive definite form
   (our case after averaging) the isotropic case never occurs, so **c(g) = reflection length
   of g in O(d)**. Hence "rank <= k" and "product of <= k reflections" coincide exactly in
   the finite regime; they do NOT coincide for non-orthogonalisable (infinite, transvection)
   maps.
2. **k = 1 (real, finite): Coxeter classification.** A finite subgroup of O(d) generated by
   reflections is a finite Coxeter group (types A_n, B_n, D_n, E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, H_3, H_4,
   I_2(m) and products). So **k_min(G,A) = 1 iff there is a finite real reflection group W
   and reflections r_a in W generating W with an epimorphism W -> G, r_a |-> a.** Necessary:
   every letter is an involution or trivial in G. Also necessary (DERIVED): every nonabelian
   composition factor of G lies in {A_n, A_5 (from H_3, H_4), U_4(2) ~ PSp_4(3) (E_6),
   Sp_6(2) (E_7), O_8^+(2) (E_8)}; composition factors from memory, UNVERIFIED.
3. **k = 1 over C: Shephard-Todd.** Finite complex reflection groups = infinite family
   G(m,p,n) plus 34 exceptional groups G_4 ... G_37. Hypothesis: field C, unitary (automatic
   for finite groups), reflections of any finite order. Realification turns a complex
   reflection into a real rotation (c_R = 2), so k_R <= 2 k_C and k_C <= k_R.
4. **k = 2 (real, finite): Lange-Mikhailova Theorem 1.** Every irreducible finite rotation
   group (generated by orthogonal maps with codim-2 fixed space) is, up to conjugation, one of:
   (i) W^+ for an irreducible real reflection group W; (ii) an irreducible unitary reflection
   group not the complexification of a real one, viewed in SO(2n); (iii) imprimitive
   G^+(km,k,l), n = 2l > 4; (iv) extensions W^+_x for W of type A_4, D_4, F_4, A_5, E_6;
   (v) groups with only order-2 rotations: monomial M_5..M_8, M_7p, M_8p, R_5(A_5) in SO(5),
   R_6(PSL_2(7)) in SO(6), and a primitive group in SO(8) that is an extension of A_8 by a
   nonabelian group of order 27; (vi) remaining rotation groups in SO(4). Theorem 2 reduces
   reflection-rotation groups to this list plus reflection groups. Caveat stated by the
   authors: reducible reflection-rotation groups need not split as products of irreducible
   ones. Consequence: **k_min <= 2 is decidable from a finite (if long) list**, modulo the
   reducible non-split cases.
5. **Carter's lemma (type-free form on Weyl groups).** In a Weyl group acting on its
   reflection representation, rank(w - I) = absolute (reflection) length l_T(w). Within
   H = W this gives exact per-letter costs.

(c) Only hypothesis (does not follow):
* That the abstract problem intends the finite regime.
* That optimal H can be taken irreducible: for k = 1 yes (reflection groups split), for
  k = 2 no (Lange-Mikhailova warn about non-split reducible groups), for k >= 3 unknown.
* There is no classification of groups generated in codimension 3 or more that I found;
  Martino-Singh only treat the lattice side and GL_3(R). The k >= 3 regime is open.

(d) Testable predictions (finite regime, field R unless stated):
* P1. G = Z/m, A = {generator}: k = 1 for m <= 2, k = 2 for m >= 3 (an order > 2 orthogonal
  element has a conjugate pair of nonreal eigenvalues). Over C: k = 1 always.
* P2. G = A_5, A = any set of involutions generating it: k = 1, via H = W(H_3) = A_5 x Z/2,
  where reflection = -(rotation by pi). Note that G itself (3-dim rotation representation)
  only gives k = 2: an instance of question (3) where a bigger H lowers k.
* P3. G = A_5, A = {(12)(34), (12345)} or containing any 3- or 5-cycle: k = 2 (icosahedral
  rotations in SO(3); k = 1 excluded by P1-type order argument).
* P4. G = PSL(2,7), A = involutions: k_R = 2 (R_6(PSL_2(7)) in SO(6) is a rotation group;
  k_R = 1 excluded because PSL(2,7) is not a quotient of a finite Coxeter group), but
  **k_C = 1** via Shephard-Todd G_24 = Z/2 x PSL(2,7), order 336 (seen in search results).
  A clean field-dependence test.
* P5. G = S_4, A = {(12), (1234)}: k = 2 (S_4 as rotation group of the cube: transposition
  = rotation by pi, 4-cycle = rotation by pi/2). This beats the "n - 1" of the permutation
  representation. G = S_5, A = {(12), (12345)}: prediction k = 4 (character computation: in
  every faithful real irreducible of S_5 the 5-cycle has codim 4; S_5 x {+-1} twists do not
  help). Lower bound k >= 3 should follow from Lange-Mikhailova (no rotation group in the
  list maps a generating rotation onto a 5-cycle of S_5) -- requires checking the reducible
  non-split cases.
* P6 (computable invariant, DERIVED upper bound). For a faithful real representation V of G
  and a sign choice e_a in {+1,-1}:
  k_min(G,A) <= min_V max_a min( dim V - dim E_{+1}(a), dim V - dim E_{-1}(a) ),
  subject to (z,-1) not in <(a,e_a)> whenever rho(z) = -I. Computable from the character
  table: dim E_{+1}(g) = (1/|g|) sum_j chi(g^j), dim E_{-1}(g) = (1/|g|) sum_j (-1)^j chi(g^j).
  Over C one may twist by any root of unity.

## Domain 2: rigid-body rotations and free subgroups of Lie groups

Literature seen:
* Epstein, "Almost all subgroups of a Lie group are free", J. Algebra 19 (1971) 261-262
  (DOI not seen; UNVERIFIED DOI).
* Sanov's theorem (free group from two integer transvections), seen in search results via
  arXiv:1605.05226 ("On two-generator subgroups in SL_2(Z), SL_2(Q), and SL_2(R)").
* Tits alternative: only a lecture-notes PDF seen; Tits' paper itself UNVERIFIED.

(a) Shared principle. Generic tuples of rotations (or transvections) satisfy no relations,
so a few low-cost generators can "track" the free group, and every G is a quotient of it.

(b) What follows: Epstein: for a connected non-solvable Lie group, almost every m-tuple
(in Haar measure) generates a free group of rank m. Apply to SO(3), where every nontrivial
element has c = 2: **in the orthogonal, infinite-image regime, k_min <= 2 for every G and A,
d = 3.** Sanov: in the unrestricted regime, k_min = 1, d = 2.

(c) Only hypothesis: that infinite images are acceptable. Free-group tracking separates
states only by arbitrarily small distances (SO(3) case) or by exponentially growing norms
(transvection case), so it is useless under finite precision. This domain gives the upper
envelope of the problem, not its interesting part.

(d) Prediction: for any G with a letter of order >= 3, orthogonal and infinite image allowed,
k_min = 2 with d = 3; with finite image, Z/7 with one letter still has k = 2 but PSL(2,7) with
{order 2, order 7} letters has k_R <= 6 (6-dim representation: the order-7 element has no fixed
vector, so codim 6; DERIVED) and k_C <= 3 (3-dim complex representation, eigenvalues
zeta, zeta^2, zeta^4). The gap between 2 (infinite) and up to 6 (finite) is a measurable cost
of demanding finiteness.

## Domain 3: reversible circuits, permutation automata and cascade decompositions

Literature seen:
* Krohn, Rhodes, "Algebraic theory of machines. I. Prime decomposition theorem for finite
  semigroups and machines", Trans. AMS 116 (1965) 450-464 (AMS URL
  S0002-9947-1965-0188316-1).
* Barrington, "Bounded-width polynomial-size branching programs recognize exactly those
  languages in NC1", STOC 1986, DOI 10.1145/12130.12131.

(a) Shared principle. A permutation of basis states has cost c(pi) = (number of points) -
(number of cycles) = transposition length; a gate that swaps exactly two basis states
(a multiply-controlled NOT, e.g. Toffoli on 3 bits) is a reflection. Wreath and cascade
products are the canonical "track a larger group and read off a quotient" constructions.

(b) What follows (DERIVED from the permutation structure, exact): for permutation
representations c(pi) = n - cyc(pi), so the permutation-restricted optimum is
k_perm(G,A) = min over faithful (mod kernel) actions of H ->> G of max_a (n - cyc(a)).
For a wreath product H_1 wr_Omega H_2 acting on V_1^Omega: a base element supported on one
coordinate costs c_1(h); a top permutation sigma costs dim(V_1) * (|Omega| - cyc(sigma)).
So cascades keep k small only if the top group moves few blocks per letter.
Krohn-Rhodes: every finite automaton divides a cascade of simple groups and resets; it
says nothing about ranks.

(c) Only hypothesis: that permutation (monomial) realisations are near-optimal. They are
not in general: Z/m needs m - 1 as a permutation but 2 orthogonally. Barrington's theorem
(non-solvable groups give NC1-hardness at width 5) is about depth and width, not rank;
I do not transfer it.

(d) Prediction: B_n = Z/2 wr S_n with alphabet {one sign flip, adjacent transpositions}:
k = 1 (it is a Coxeter group) even though the group is exponentially large; Z/3 wr S_n with
{one coordinate rotation, transpositions}: k_R = 2, k_C = 1 (G(3,1,n) is a complex
reflection group). Computable gap test: compare k_perm and P6 bound on small groups.

## Domain 4: invariant theory and orbifold quotients

Literature seen:
* Kac, Watanabe, "Finite linear groups whose ring of invariants is a complete
  intersection", Bull. AMS 6 (1982) 221-223, DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-1982-14989-8.
* Lange, "Characterization of finite groups generated by reflections and rotations",
  arXiv:1509.06771, J. Topology 9 (2016) 1109-1129 (abstract only).
* Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem: standard; the specific primary source for Chevalley
  UNVERIFIED.

(a) Shared principle. "Generated by elements of small codimension" is detected by
regularity of the quotient V/H: codim 1 <-> polynomial invariants, codim <= 2 <-> complete
intersection invariants (one direction), PL-manifold quotients.

(b) What follows: Kac-Watanabe (finite H in GL(V), V over C): if C[V]^H is a complete
intersection then H is generated by elements with codim Fix <= 2. Chevalley-Shephard-Todd
(char 0): C[V]^H polynomial iff H generated by pseudo-reflections. These give computable
certificates (Molien series, Gröbner bases) that a candidate H has cost <= 2 or 1.

(c) Only hypothesis: the converse of Kac-Watanabe is false in general, so non-CI does not
prove k > 2. These certificates test a given H, not the minimum over all H ->> G.

(d) Prediction: for candidate H from P4/P5 the invariant ring of R_6(PSL_2(7)) (real
6-dim) should be checked; a CI invariant ring would certify k_C <= 2 for PSL(2,7).

## Cross-domain structural results for question (2) (DERIVED unless cited)

* Order obstruction: a letter whose image in G has order > 2 forces k >= 2 (real, finite).
* **Counting lemma:** for every nontrivial irreducible constituent W of V,
  sum_a codim W^{M_a} >= dim W (the common fixed space of the generators is H-invariant,
  hence 0). So k >= dim W / |A|.
* **Feit-Tits bound (Q3 restriction):** Feit, Tits, "Projective representations of minimum
  degree of group extensions", Canad. J. Math. 30 (1978) 1092-1102 (seen; DOI not seen).
  Result as summarised: a nontrivial projective representation of minimal degree of a finite
  extension of a nonabelian simple group S factors through S, except some groups of Lie type
  in characteristic 2. Combined with the counting lemma: if S is a nonabelian composition
  factor of G and R(S) its minimal nontrivial projective degree in characteristic 0, then
  **k_min(G,A) >= ceil(R(S) / |A|), and passing to a bigger H cannot beat this** (modulo the
  listed exceptions; exact hypotheses must be read in the paper). Example: A_n or S_n with
  a 2-letter alphabet, n >= 9: k >= ceil((n-1)/2) (R(A_n) = n - 1 for n >= 9 is from memory,
  UNVERIFIED). So small k with a small alphabet is impossible for groups with large simple
  factors; small k is possible with large alphabets (A_n with all 3-cycles: k = 2).
* What allows small k: G is a quotient of a reflection or rotation group with letters going
  to reflections or rotations; exceptional isomorphisms (A_5 ~ icosahedral, S_4 ~ octahedral,
  PSL(2,7) ~ Klein) produce anomalously small k.
* Answer pattern for question (3): yes, a bigger H can lower k (A_5 -> W(H_3): 2 -> 1;
  PSL(2,7) -> G_24 over C: 2 -> 1), mostly via central or scalar twists; Feit-Tits caps
  the gain at the dimension-counting bound.

Near-target note (not a source domain, flagged so the team can de-duplicate): Siems et al.,
arXiv:2502.10297, state (per the fetched HTML) that S_n is handled in one layer with n_h =
n - 1 Householder factors, consistent with the n - 1 upper bound from the permutation
representation; P5 (S_4 with k = 2) shows n - 1 is not always optimal.

## Formal stage: the analogy as a span (Domain 1 -> target)

Common small theory T ("costed generated groups"):
* Sorts: group H, finite generating family (s_a)_{a in A}, real inner-product space V,
  faithful homomorphism rho: H -> O(V), epimorphism p: H -> G with p(s_a) = a.
* Cost c(h) := codim Fix(rho(h)).
* Axioms:
  T1 (subadditivity) c(gh) <= c(g) + c(h); T2 (conjugation invariance) c(x g x^-1) = c(g);
  T3 (additivity over direct sums of V); T4 (reflection characterisation) c(h) = 1 iff rho(h)
  is an orthogonal reflection; T5 (Scherk) c(h) = minimal number of reflections whose
  product is rho(h); T6 (counting) sum_a c_W(s_a) >= dim W for each nontrivial irreducible
  constituent W; T7 (finiteness) H finite.

Source model: finite real reflection and rotation groups (Coxeter, Lange-Mikhailova),
with complex reflection groups via realification. Target model: the symbol-driven system
with H = <M_a>.

Proof obligations in the target (what must be verified for transfer to be valid):
1. O1 Finiteness: H = <M_a> is finite (or the problem statement is restricted to that case).
   Without it T4, T5, T7 fail (transvections: c = 1 but not reflections; Sanov breaks all
   lower bounds).
2. O2 Orthogonalisation: an H-invariant positive definite inner product exists (follows
   from O1 by averaging) and the problem is invariant under change of basis (rank is).
3. O3 Field: target is over R; any complex-source statement needs realification (c doubles
   for complex reflections) -- only k_R <= 2 k_C transfers, not equality.
4. O4 Readout condition: existence of h0 with Stab_H(h0) <= ker p (generic h0 works when
   H is finite) -- this is what turns "state determines the G-element" into "p exists".
5. O5 Generators-to-letters: the letters must be sent to the given generators, not to
   arbitrary elements; classification results must be applied to the generating family,
   not merely the abstract group (W generated by its reflections, not just isomorphic).
6. O6 For k = 2 transfer: handle reducible non-split rotation groups separately
   (Lange-Mikhailova caveat).

Transfer class:
* Source -> target, constructive (existential, any k): every explicit source realisation
  (a reflection/rotation group with an epimorphism onto G respecting generators) is a
  target solution; these give UPPER bounds and are valid once O1-O5 hold.
* Source -> target, classificatory (universal, k = 1 and k = 2 only): "every finite group
  generated in codimension 1 (resp. 2) is on the list" gives LOWER bounds / impossibility
  results (e.g. k_R(PSL(2,7), involutions) != 1). Valid only under O1, O2, O3 (real), O6.
* Universal Horn consequences of T1-T6 (counting bound, order bound) transfer in both
  directions, and hold even without classification.
* Not transferable: statements depending on presentations or Coxeter diagrams beyond the
  existence of the epimorphism; anything in the infinite regime; statements about
  learnability, stability or precision.

## Rejected purely verbal analogies

* "k-local quantum gates": locality is not low rank. U tensor I on n qubits has
  rank(U tensor I - I) = rank(U - I) * 2^(n-k), so few-qubit gates are high-cost here; the
  only valid quantum analogue is basis-state transpositions (absorbed into Domain 3).
* "Few degrees of freedom" in robotics or "few moves" in puzzle groups: the number of joints
  or moves does not bound codim Fix of the induced linear map; puzzle moves are permutations
  with large support. Only the permutation cost formula transfers (Domain 3).
* "Low-weight errors" in coding theory: Hamming weight of an error vector is not the rank of
  a linear map's deviation from identity; no shared theorem.
* Crystallographic point groups: genuine but not a new domain (finite subgroups of O(3)),
  subsumed by Domain 1.
* Barrington-style "non-solvable means hard": concerns width and depth of programs, not the
  rank of each step; it gives no bound on k (A_5 already has k = 1 with involution letters).
