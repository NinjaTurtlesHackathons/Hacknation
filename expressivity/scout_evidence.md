## 2. Precise claims of linear-RNN state-tracking papers

Notation: GH = generalized Householder I - beta k k^T with ||k|| = 1; "eigenvalue range [-1,1]" means beta in [0,2]; M_k^n(Omega) = products of k GH matrices whose non-trivial eigenvalue lies in Omega (Grazzi eq. 3). n_h = number of Householder factors per token in DeltaProduct. All results are for finite precision unless noted, with arbitrary (MLP) decoders and, for multi-layer constructions, MLPs that may be very large.

### Grazzi et al., arXiv:2411.12537 (ICLR 2025) -- body text from arXiv HTML (latest version)

- Lower bound, Thm 1 (Parity), Sec. 4.1: a finite-precision LRNN with finitely many layers recognizes (11)* / parity at all lengths "only if in at least one layer, there exist ${\bm{x}}$ such that ${\bm{A}}({\bm{x}})$ has at least one eigenvalue $\lambda\notin\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,x\geq 0\}$". Applies to diagonal and non-diagonal (e.g. DeltaNet with beta in [0,1]). Generalizes Sarrof et al. Thm 2/3.2.
- Lower bound, Thm 2 (Modular counting), Sec. 4.1: an L-layer finite-precision LRNN counts modulo m (m not a power of two) only if, for some layer i, a product of 2^(i-1) transition matrices has an eigenvalue with nonzero imaginary part. Consequence stated in the paper: diagonal or triangular real matrices cannot count mod 3, even with negative eigenvalues.
- Lower bound / structural, Prop. 1.3: matrices in M_k^n((-1,1]) have eigenvalues equal to 1 or |lambda| < 1; for M_k^n([0,1]) with k <= 2 all eigenvalues are in [0,1]. Hence standard DeltaNet (beta in [0,1]) cannot be a reflection or rotation and cannot solve parity.
- Expressivity of GH products, Prop. 1.2: every matrix with ||M|| <= 1 lies in M_{3n}^n([-1,1]); every orthogonal matrix lies in M_n^n({-1,1}); every permutation matrix lies in M_{n-1}^n({-1,1}). Proof cites the Cartan-Dieudonne theorem ("every $n\times n$ orthogonal matrix can be written as a product of $n$ reflections").
- Construction, Thm 3 (one layer): every FSA whose transition monoid is a group is implemented by a one-layer finite-precision LRNN with A in M_{k-1}^n({-1,1}), n = smallest degree of a faithful permutation representation (subgroup of S_n), k = maximal number of states moved by one input letter. Cyclic group Z_m: A in M_2^2([-1,1]) (2x2 rotations, two Householders). Parity: A in {-1,1} (scalar). Corollary stated in the body: transitions that are swaps or identity need only one GH (DeltaNet with beta up to 2).
- Construction, Thm 6 (App. D, "modular addition with reflections"): two layers, n_h = 1 (reflections only) suffice for addition modulo m for any m: layer 1 is a scalar -1 recurrence (parity of position), layer 2 uses 2x2 reflections with 2m states.
- Construction, Thm 4 (regular languages): every FSA is implemented by an LRNN with s <= 2^|Q| layers, width n <= |Q|, A in M_n^n([-1,1]); proof via Krohn-Rhodes (permutation-reset cascade, one cascade component per layer; zero matrix for resets obtained inefficiently as product of n GH, motivating gates). Authors "conjecture that the number of layers might be reduced to at most linear using a more refined decomposition".
- Discussion (body, after Thm 4): contrasts with transformers and diagonal LRNNs which, at constant depth and bounded precision, "can only implement an FSA if all groups in its transition monoid are solvable, i.e. excluding groups isomorphic to $S_{n}$ with $n\geq 5$".
- No lower bound on the number of Householder factors needed for a specific group (S_n, A_n, D_n) is proved; only the eigenvalue-type necessary conditions above.
- Open question stated (Limitations): whether multi-layer DeltaNet[-1,1] (n_h = 1) can solve S_n word problems for n >= 3 (they only showed Z_m with 2 layers).
- Empirical: Mamba[-1,1] and DeltaNet[-1,1] solve parity; improved mod arithmetic and S3/S5-type tasks; 1.3B DeltaNet[-1,1] trains stably. Negative eigenvalues break log-space cumulative-product tricks of Mamba-2/GLA/mLSTM.

### Siems et al. (DeltaProduct), arXiv:2502.10297 -- body text from arXiv HTML (latest version)

- Architecture: A(x_i) = prod_{j=1..n_h} (I - beta_{i,j} k_{i,j} k_{i,j}^T), beta in [0,2]; Gated DeltaProduct multiplies by scalar gate g_i in [0,1]. ||A|| <= 1 always (stability).
- Rotations/reflections (Sec. 3, "Expressivity of Householder products"): "DeltaProduct's can model any orthogonal matrix. This is a consequence of the Cartan-Dieudonné theorem, which establishes that any $n\times n$ orthogonal matrix can be expressed as a product of at most $n$ reflections". Figure 3: two reflections give a 2D rotation by 2*theta. Prop. 1: identical keys give no extra expressivity; orthogonal keys give identity + symmetric rank-n_h; complex eigenvalues need non-trivially linearly dependent keys. With n_h = 1, "rotation matrices cannot be represented" (App. B, proof of Thm 7).
- Thm 1 (= App. Thm 3), S_n word problem, constructions: (i) one layer with n_h = n-1 (from Grazzi Thm 3); (ii) 3 layers with n_h > 1; (iii) 4 layers with n_h = 1. For (ii)/(iii) the second-to-last-layer MLP computes a lookup table of size 2m x (n!)^{2m}, m = ceil((n-1)/n_h). A single DeltaProduct layer can only realize permutations moving at most n_h + 1 elements (from the norm bound, App. B.6). Since any finite group embeds in some S_n, this implies every group word problem is solvable (with the above layer counts). Answers Grazzi's open question constructively for n_h = 1 with 4 layers.
- Thm 2 (= App. Thm 5): for any n_h >= 1 and any regular language, a Gated DeltaProduct with finitely many layers (language-dependent) recognizes it; Krohn-Rhodes cascade with at most 4 layers per permutation-reset component.
- App. Thm 4 (orthogonal groups): if G is isomorphic to a subgroup of O(n), or to a subgroup of SO(n+1) with n even, then n_h = n suffices for a one-layer solution (state H_0 = I, A(x) = G_x, B = 0). SO(n+1) case uses that det +1 elements are products of an even number (<= n) of reflections.
- App. Thm 7 (dihedral D_m): (i) two layers, n_h = 1 (>= 2 heads in layer 1, computing parities of reflections and rotations); (ii) one layer with n_h >= 2 (D_m is a finite subgroup of O(2)).
- Lemma 2: counting modulo d with 2 layers n_h = 1 (from Grazzi Thm 6) or 1 layer with n_h >= 2.
- Table 1 summary (layers x architecture): 1 layer -- DeltaNet and RWKV-7: S_n with only 2-permutations as inputs; DeltaProduct n_h > 1: S_n with only (n_h+1)-permutations, finite subgroups of O(n_h), SO(n_h+1) if n_h even. 2 layers -- Z_n, D_n (all three). 3 layers -- DeltaProduct: S_n with lookup size 2m x (n!)^{2m}. 4 layers -- DeltaNet: S_n with lookup 2(n-1) x (n!)^{2(n-1)}; RWKV-7: regular languages. Gated variants: regular languages with f(|Q|) layers.
- Thm 6 (App. B.4): replacing Householders by RWKV-7 matrices (c = 2, copy matrices with norm sqrt 2) gives one layer with n_h = n for any n-state FSA; 3 layers with n_h > 1; 4 layers with n_h = 1 (from Peng et al. Thm 3). Thm 8: RWKV-7 with c = 1 and varying a_i admits pairs with spectral radius of product > 1.2, i.e. unstable.
- Empirical (Sec. 5): S3 needs n_h = 2 and S5 needs n_h = 4 for reliable extrapolation beyond length 128 (matching n-1 of Thm 3); "Unexpectedly, $S_{4}$ and $A_{5}$ can extrapolate robustly using only $n_{h}=2$ despite the theorem suggesting 3 and 4, respectively. This efficiency arises from their isomorphism to subgroups of $\mathrm{SO}(3,\mathbb{R})$ , i.e. the group of 3D rotations". Multi-layer DeltaNet[-1,1] (n_h = 1): S3 needed 3 layers, S4 6, A5 3, S5 not solved with 10 layers, although theory gives 2 layers for S3 and 4 for any group.
- Lower bounds: none. The paper gives only sufficient constructions; it does not prove that S5 needs n_h >= 4 in one layer, nor that n_h = 1 cannot do S_n in fewer than 4 layers. Implicitly, the norm bound restricts a single factor-product to permutation matrices moving <= n_h + 1 points, but this is a statement about permutation-matrix representations, not about all representations (the SO(3) case shows other representations need fewer factors).
- Open question stated (Sec. 4.1): "It is an open question whether there exists a continuous parameterization of state-transition matrices which yields stable recurrences and still allows to recognize any regular language in a finite and fixed number of layers." Future work: adaptive number of Householders per token.

### Peng et al. (RWKV-7), arXiv:2503.14456 -- body text, App. D

- Lemma 2: one-layer RWKV-7 tracks swaps on 5 elements when each token encodes one transposition (outputs whether the product is identity). Thm 2: hence RWKV-7 solves an NC1-complete problem under AC0 reductions (beyond TC0 if TC0 != NC1). Note: input format is transpositions, not arbitrary S5 elements.
- Thm 3: every regular language is recognized by a 4-layer RWKV-7 (uses DFA transition matrices decomposed into swap/copy/identity factors, Lemma 3). Thm 1: under the practical clamping regime the matrix is similar to a symmetric matrix (real spectrum).
- Empirical: minimum layers for > 95% accuracy on A5, A4 x Z5, Z60 (Merrill et al. setup); RWKV-7 better than Transformer, Mamba, S4, weaker than classical RNN.
- Lower bounds: none beyond citing TC0 limits for other architectures.

### Merrill, Petty, Sabharwal, arXiv:2404.08819

- Upper bound: log-precision non-gated, diagonal input-dependent, and simultaneously diagonalizable SSMs are in L-uniform TC0 (Thms 4.2, 4.4, 4.6); so S5 word problem impossible unless TC0 = NC1. Holds for any constant number of layers.
- Construction: one-layer RNN-SSM and one-layer IDS4 (input-dependent, non-diagonal) recognize every regular language (Thm 5.1, 5.2).
- Open question (Intro): "It is an open question whether such SSM architectures with greater expressivity for state tracking are practically viable for large-scale language modeling."

### Sarrof, Veitsman, Hahn, arXiv:2405.17394

- Nonnegative-gate SSMs at finite precision: cannot recognize PARITY (Thm 3.2); predictively model exactly the star-free regular languages (Thm 3.4). Positive results for flip-flop (2 layers), bounded Dyck (2 layers, d = O(h log K)), counter languages.

### Shakerinava et al., arXiv:2603.01959

- Input-dependent complex diagonal SSMs, finite precision: 1 layer tracks exactly the abelian groups; k layers track G iff G has a subnormal series of length k with abelian factors. Non-solvable groups (A5, S5) at no depth. This is a true lower bound (iff) for diagonal complex transitions. Empirical learnability gap: multi-layer models fail to learn S3, A4.

### Khavari et al., arXiv:2508.07395

- Diagonal SSMs: stacking input-independent layers (any eigenvalues) with input-dependent nonnegative layers still cannot solve parity; a single layer must be both input-dependent and have negative eigenvalues.

### Other follow-ups relating Householders, reflections, rotations

- Howe, arXiv:2609.18966 (empirical, pre-registered): proposes a "representation law": min Householders per token = maximal reflection length of the generators in the format-pinned representation (parity 1, S4 3, A5 and S5 4); explicitly contrasts with DeltaProduct's n_h = 2 for S4/A5 via SO(3), concluding the law is representation-relative and task format is a hidden variable. Not a theorem.
- Larson, arXiv:2609.12259 (empirical): learned matrix-memory rank equals minimal faithful real representation dimension d_min; S4 and A5 (both d_min = 3) indistinguishable.
- Fujii, arXiv:2609.28273 (empirical): Mamba-3 diagonal + input-dependent rank-1 reflection enables non-commutative tracking.
- Karuvally, arXiv:2507.05238 (theorem): adaptive unitary (skew-symmetric generator) SSM simulates solvable group automata at log precision.
- Yang, arXiv:2505.16381 (PaTH): Householder products as data-dependent position encoding in attention (empirical).

## 3. Open questions stated in the literature

1. Stable yet fully expressive parameterization: "whether there exists a continuous parameterization of state-transition matrices which yields stable recurrences and still allows to recognize any regular language in a finite and fixed number of layers" (Siems et al., arXiv:2502.10297, Sec. 4.1).
2. Layer count of GH-product LRNNs for regular languages: Grazzi et al. need up to 2^|Q| layers and "conjecture that the number of layers might be reduced to at most linear using a more refined decomposition" (arXiv:2411.12537, discussion after Thm 4).
3. Multi-layer DeltaNet[-1,1]: "we do not know if it can also solve other word problems, such as the ones for the symmetric groups $S_{n}$ with $n\geq 3$" (Grazzi et al., Limitations). Partly answered constructively by Siems et al. (4 layers, huge lookup MLP), but no lower bound on depth exists and empirically S5 fails with 10 layers.
4. Hypothesized trade-off between state tracking and associative recall (Grazzi et al., Limitations).
5. Adaptive number of Householders per token (Siems et al., Future work).
6. Practical viability of more expressive SSMs for large-scale LM (Merrill et al., arXiv:2404.08819, Intro).
7. Expressivity vs learnability gap: theoretical constructions exist but training fails (Shakerinava et al., arXiv:2603.01959 for S3/A4 with diagonal multi-layer; Siems et al. for multi-layer n_h = 1; Sarrof et al. call for studying flat minima / gradient flow; Deletang et al.).
8. Finer understanding of SSM vs transformer vs RNN power "remains an open question" (Sarrof et al., Intro).
9. Extending saturated-RNN hierarchy results to unsaturated RNNs (Merrill et al., arXiv:2004.08500).
10. Finite-precision robustness: exact-arithmetic constructions may not survive scan perturbations / error accumulation (Brandoit, arXiv:2609.32447; Chung, arXiv:2605.07755), and expressivity depends on the arithmetic model (Nowak, arXiv:2606.01765).
11. Gap noted by the scout (not stated as a theorem anywhere in the fetched literature): no paper fetched proves a lower bound on the number of Householder factors per token (or per layer) for a given group word problem; the only quantitative per-token relation is the empirical law of Howe (arXiv:2609.18966).
