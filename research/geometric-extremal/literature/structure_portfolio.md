## Six concrete questions

### S1 — Minimum distance ratio for 16 planar points

Definition: minimize R(P)=max_{i<j} ||p_i-p_j||² / min_{i<j} ||p_i-p_j||² over 16 distinct points in R². Normalize minimum squared distance to 1, not the enclosing-circle radius. Baseline: **12.88924**, rounded published value in [Berthold et al., arXiv:2601.05943, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05943), January 2026; supersedes 12.88927 from AlphaEvolve. Exact coordinate retrieval and higher-precision baseline are required before claiming improvement. Other freshly improved planar cases: n=21 (17.77499), n=22 (19.05398), n=29 (25.92460). Search evaluator O(n²), negligible at these n. Generic nonlinear Xpress/SCIP already compete successfully; another ordinary multistart alone is weak differentiation. Attack: identify simultaneous minimum/maximum-distance contact graph, change discrete graph, optimize its embedding, then release symmetry. Rational coordinates certify a strict ratio improvement by exact squared distances, provided margin survives rationalization. Visual: nearest and farthest edges in different colors. Family prospects: moderate, graph motifs may recur. **Priority: balanced backup**, weak novelty risk from rounded baselines.

### S2 — Larger perimeter of an equilateral small hexadecagon

Definition: maximize perimeter of a convex 16-gon of diameter ≤1 with all consecutive edge lengths equal. The published A16 witness has **3.1352878881**, versus universal upper bound **3.1365484905**, in [Bingane–Audet 2022, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.10618). Beware n=32: their later Z32 witness is **3.1403202339**, better than A32. Author coordinates: [OPTIGON](https://github.com/cbingane/optigon), MATLAB/Julia, MIT. New competing work: [Guo–Luo, arXiv:2608.08001](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08001), August 2026, presents explicitly provisional computer-assisted proof candidates for unrestricted n=16,32,64 maximum perimeter. It requires a full relevance audit; unrestricted optimality does not directly give equilateral optimality. Search O(n²), convexity and equal-edge constraints explicit. Attack: rewire diameter graph while preserving equal-side closure; compare symmetric and nonsymmetric branches. Certify equal lengths algebraically or with interval root existence, convexity and every diameter independently. Arbitrarily rationalized coordinates generally lose equilateralness. Visual: regular polygon → A16 → candidate, diameter chords and side equality. Family prospects high at powers of two. **Priority: ambitious structured search**, baseline first.

### S3 — Larger width of an equilateral small hexadecagon

Definition: maximize minimum supporting-line separation of a convex equilateral 16-gon with diameter ≤1. [Bingane–Audet, Mathematics of Computation 2022, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.00036) reports **W(G16)=0.9940673080**, upper **cos(π/32)=0.9951847267**; n=32 baseline **0.9987316811**. Its exact octagon problem is already solved; repeating n=8 is infrastructure only. OPTIGON implements the family. Fast evaluator computes minimum width in edge-normal directions, O(n²), after strict convexity; evaluate all pairs for diameter. Attack: vary height/contact graph outside the G family, then remove its reflection constraint. Equal sides require interval-Newton or exact algebraic root existence. Width constraints can be certified by positive oriented cross products and distances to each opposite supporting vertex, avoiding square roots through squared comparisons. Visual width calipers explain the objective immediately. A better asymptotic deficit coefficient across n=16,32,64 would be more significant than a one-off decimal gain. **Priority: top continuous direction**, approximately 0.0011 gap offers more room than unrestricted perimeter.

### S4 — A 61-edge unit-distance graph on 22 planar vertices

Definition: 22 distinct planar points, maximize number of pairs at exactly Euclidean distance 1; crossings are allowed and no minimum-distance exclusion is imposed. [Alexeev–Mixon–Parshall, arXiv:2412.11914v2, 12 February 2025, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11914v2): **60≤u(22)≤61**. All n≤21 are solved. A 61-edge witness would solve the first open case. Known methods: forbidden subgraphs, Moser ring construction and custom algebraic embedding; complete 61-edge graph enumeration was estimated at 15,000 CPU hours. Search alternative: add/delete vertices in Moser-ring sets plus non-ring algebraic embeddings; do not launch unrestricted graph enumeration on a laptop. Exact coordinates in a number field with all edge squared lengths 1 and pairwise injectivity are straightforward independent certificates; approximate 1 is insufficient. Evaluation O(n²), significance very high, success probability low. Contact drawing compelling, extension to n=23 gives a family test. **Priority: high-ambition reserve**.

### S5 — More than 27 planar points with at most 12 distances

Definition: g(12)=max{|P|:P⊂R², number of distinct nonzero pairwise distances ≤12}; not restricted to a lattice. [Bao–Yu, arXiv:2509.00880](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00880), 2025 with current revised source, lists **27** for k=12, **37** for k=16, and **48** for k=19. These are lower bounds, not global solutions. Author code: [triangular-m-distance](https://github.com/BaoCoder613/triangular-m-distance). Lattice positions (a+b/2,√3 b/2) have squared norms a²+ab+b², hence integer exact checks O(n²). Search the distance palette itself, not just the first k norms: mixed maximum-clique/SAT and add/delete point search. Their source already shows first-k palettes lose against larger-distance substitutions. Success: 28 points with ≤12 distinct norms, or 38 with ≤16; then audit all current unrestricted constructions before asserting a global record. Family: repeated low-multiplicity-distance removal mechanism. Visually color pairs by distance and show palette. **Priority: top exact/discrete direction**. g(6)=13 is already established/classified and is rejected. g(7)>16 is an ambitious alternate. [Szöllősi–Östergård 2020](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v27i1p23) supplies graph/Gröbner methods. The 2025 preprint [2510.09800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09800) contains an abstract-level nonmatching-bound/asymptotic-equivalence inconsistency; do not import its claimed asymptotic conclusion.

### S6 — Cover the unit square by 25 smaller equal disks

Definition: minimize r with [0,1]²⊆∪_{i=1}^{25}B(c_i,r), centers unconstrained (projection into the square cannot worsen distance). Original report [Nurmela–Östergård, HUT-TCS-A62, June 2000](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/covering-a-square-with-up-to-30-equal-circles/), Table 1, gives **r=0.13354870656077049693…**. Full report text was fetched [here](https://paperzz.com/doc/9410076/covering-a-square-with-up-to-30-equal-circles). The current-public numerical coordinate reconstruction can differ by ~2e-10 and is not a certified new record. Later-record search found no primary improvement; this is a search limitation, not proof that this is current-best. Algorithms: BFGS uncovered-area minimization, then contact/equidistant-graph reconstruction. Stronger attack: optimize maximum clipped-Voronoi-vertex distance rather than area, rewire Delaunay contacts near boundary defects, release reflection. Candidate evaluation O(n log n); rigorous checker must inspect all clipped Voronoi vertices, all boundary intersections and corners. Rational centers plus exact rational Voronoi clipping and squared distances certify coverage of entire continuous square, whereas a dense grid cannot. Visual: uncovered sliver magnification with Voronoi witness. Family plausible via boundary-row defects. **Priority: baseline-provenance blocked**, useful verifier development.

## Two concrete recommended attacks and gates

1. **S5 distance-palette evolution.** Reproduce the 27-point k=12 construction exactly. Build independent integer norm checker and a negative control adding a forbidden norm. Enumerate palette replacements among the first 30 triangular norms, anchor a pair, maximum clique over compatible lattice sites; independently use incremental point-set edits scored by total distinct norms. Success is an exact ≥28-point witness. Escalate to k=16,19,23 if k=12 stalls; archive every palette and best set. Stop repeating identical palettes; use distance multiplicities to replace low-value norms. No global-optimality claim from finite lattice search.

2. **S3 height-graph search.** Reproduce G16 and regular16 widths. Implement evaluator with explicit edge equality, convexity, diameter and support constraints, and deliberately perturb an edge/diameter negative control. One team optimizes contact topology; another releases symmetry in vertex coordinates with equality constraints. A candidate needs width separated from baseline and a square nonsingular defining system for independent interval root certification. Extend a successful motif to n=32. Stop raw multistart after repeated recovery of G16; change graph or parametrization. If equality certification fails, preserve candidate only as observed, never a record.

## Source audit update, 2026-10-04

- S4 is now **paused for novelty audit**: root located a September 2026 project [The Sixty-First Distance](https://artwaste.land/strata/the-sixty-first-distance/) claiming u(22)=60 through complete enumeration. This claim has not been independently audited here; do not describe22 as unconditionally the first open case or launch a61-edge record hunt without assessing it.
- S5 full prior-art scan found [Ahmed–Snevily 2013](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v20i4p33/pdf/), with bounds through k50, including stronger k25≥64 and k31≥80 than Bao–Yu's table. They use **exactly k** distances; our at-most formulation requires monotonizing all lower bounds. Main thresholds k12≥28,k16≥38,k19≥49,k23≥62 remain unchanged. Bladed polygon families and multiplicity-guided observations are existing prior art, not new mechanisms.
- [Ahmed–Wildstrom 2015, author PDF](https://tanbir.github.io/files/preprints/ahmed_wildstrom_distance_sets.pdf), [publication index](https://tanbir.github.io/publications.html), already proves that a cycle-bounded finite triangular-lattice set has every difference vector realized by boundary points. The symmetric-hexagon boundary-distance conjecture is therefore solved and must not be advertised as an open target. No stronger small-k construction table appears in this paper.
