0. CONVENTIONS AND PREREQUISITES

G is a finite group with identity e and order |G|. All vector spaces are finite-dimensional over the complex numbers C.
Used without proof (standard linear algebra and group theory):
  (LA1) Every linear map of a nonzero complex vector space to itself has an eigenvalue.
  (LA2) A matrix is diagonalizable if its minimal polynomial has no repeated roots.
  (LA3) tr(AB) = tr(BA); trace is linear; the trace of a diagonal matrix is the sum of its diagonal entries.
  (LA4) If V = U + U' is a direct sum, the dimensions add.
  (GT1) Conjugacy is an equivalence relation; G is the disjoint union of its conjugacy classes.
  (GT2) For a subgroup H of G, the left cosets of H partition G into |G|/|H| sets of size |H| (Lagrange).


1. DEFINITIONS

D1 (representation). A representation of G is a pair (V, rho) where V is a vector space and rho: G -> GL(V) is a group
   homomorphism: rho(gh) = rho(g) rho(h). dim V is the degree. We write gv for rho(g)v when rho is clear.
D2 (subrepresentation, irreducible). A subspace W of V is G-invariant if gW is contained in W for all g. Then W is a
   subrepresentation. V is irreducible if V != 0 and its only G-invariant subspaces are 0 and V.
D3 (G-homomorphism). A linear map f: V -> W between representations is a G-homomorphism if f(gv) = g f(v) for all g, v.
   Hom_G(V, W) is the space of these maps. V and W are isomorphic if there is a bijective G-homomorphism.
D4 (direct sum). V + W with g(v, w) = (gv, gw).
D5 (fixed points). V^G = { v in V : gv = v for all g in G }.
D6 (character). chi_V(g) = tr rho(g). The function chi_V: G -> C is the character of V.
D7 (class function, inner product). f: G -> C is a class function if f(hgh^-1) = f(g) for all g, h. For functions
   f1, f2: G -> C put  <f1, f2> = (1/|G|) sum_{g in G} f1(g) conj(f2(g)).
   On the space of class functions this is a Hermitian inner product (positive definite: <f, f> = (1/|G|) sum |f(g)|^2).
D8 (regular representation). C[G] = vector space with basis {e_x : x in G}, and g e_x = e_{gx}.
D9 (Hom representation). For representations V, W, the space Hom(V, W) of all linear maps is a representation via
   (g.f) = rho_W(g) o f o rho_V(g)^-1. Its fixed points are exactly Hom_G(V, W).
D10 (centralizer). C_G(g) = { h in G : hg = gh }, a subgroup of G.


2. LINEAR-ALGEBRA LEMMA

Lemma 1 [L-1, proved_text]
  (a) If P: V -> V is linear with P^2 = P, then tr P = dim im P.
  (b) For fixed linear maps A: W -> W and B: V -> V, the map Phi: Hom(V, W) -> Hom(V, W), Phi(f) = A f B, has
      tr Phi = tr A * tr B.
  (c) If A^m = I for some m >= 1, then A is diagonalizable, every eigenvalue lambda satisfies lambda^m = 1, and
      tr(A^-1) = conj(tr A).
Proof.
  (a) Every v equals Pv + (v - Pv), with Pv in im P and P(v - Pv) = 0, so V = im P + ker P; the sum is direct because
      v in im P means v = Pu, so Pv = P^2 u = Pu = v, and if also Pv = 0 then v = 0. In a basis adapted to this
      splitting, P = diag(1,...,1,0,...,0) with dim im P ones. Hence tr P = dim im P.
  (b) Choose bases; let E_ij be the matrix unit (1 in row i, column j). Then A E_ij B = sum_{k,l} A_ki B_jl E_kl, so the
      coefficient of E_ij in Phi(E_ij) is A_ii B_jj. Summing over i, j: tr Phi = (sum_i A_ii)(sum_j B_jj).
  (c) The minimal polynomial of A divides x^m - 1, which has the m distinct roots exp(2 pi i k/m); by (LA2) A is
      diagonalizable, A = S diag(lambda_1..lambda_n) S^-1 with lambda_k^m = 1. Then |lambda_k| = 1, so
      lambda_k^-1 = conj(lambda_k), and tr(A^-1) = sum lambda_k^-1 = sum conj(lambda_k) = conj(tr A).                    QED


3. CHARACTERS: BASIC PROPERTIES

Proposition 1 [P-1, proved_text]  For representations V, W of G and g, h in G:
  (i)   chi_V(e) = dim V;
  (ii)  chi_V(hgh^-1) = chi_V(g), i.e. chi_V is a class function;
  (iii) chi_V(g^-1) = conj(chi_V(g));
  (iv)  chi_{V+W} = chi_V + chi_W;
  (v)   isomorphic representations have equal characters;
  (vi)  the character of Hom(V, W) (D9) is g -> chi_W(g) conj(chi_V(g)).
Proof.
  (i) rho(e) = I. (ii) tr(rho(h)rho(g)rho(h)^-1) = tr rho(g) by (LA3). (iii) g^m = e for m = order of g, so
  rho(g)^m = I; apply Lemma 1(c). (iv) In a basis adapted to V + W, rho(g) is block diagonal. (v) If T: V -> W is a
  bijective G-homomorphism, rho_W(g) = T rho_V(g) T^-1; take traces. (vi) By Lemma 1(b) with A = rho_W(g),
  B = rho_V(g^-1), the trace of f -> g.f is chi_W(g) chi_V(g^-1) = chi_W(g) conj(chi_V(g)) by (iii).                   QED
Checked by: E-C1..E-S4 (characters are recomputed and tested to be class functions).


4. COMPLETE REDUCIBILITY

Theorem 1 (Maschke) [T-1, proved_text]  Every G-invariant subspace W of a representation V has a G-invariant complement
  W' (V = W + W' direct). Consequently every representation is a direct sum of irreducible representations.
Proof.
  Pick any linear projection pi: V -> V with im pi = W and pi(w) = w for w in W (extend a basis of W to a basis of V,
  send the new basis vectors to 0). Define
      P = (1/|G|) sum_{g in G} rho(g) pi rho(g)^-1.
  For w in W and every g: rho(g)^-1 w is in W, so pi rho(g)^-1 w = rho(g)^-1 w and the summand gives w; hence Pw = w.
  For v in V, pi rho(g)^-1 v is in W and rho(g) maps W into W; hence im P is in W. Together im P = W and P^2 = P.
  For h in G: rho(h) P rho(h)^-1 = (1/|G|) sum_g rho(hg) pi rho(hg)^-1 = P (reindex g -> hg). So P is a
  G-homomorphism and W' = ker P is G-invariant (P(gv) = gPv = 0). By Lemma 1(a)'s splitting, V = im P + ker P = W + W'.
  Second statement: induction on dim V. If V = 0 it is the empty sum; if V is irreducible we are done; otherwise take a
  G-invariant W with 0 != W != V and apply the induction hypothesis to W and to its invariant complement W'.          QED
Checked by: E-M (the averaged projection is computed for S3 acting on Q^3, starting from a non-invariant projection).

Example E-M [computed_rigorous]  Let S3 act on C^3 by permuting coordinates, W = span(1,1,1), and let E be the projection
  onto W along span(e1, e2) (matrix with all three rows equal to (0,0,1)). E is not a G-homomorphism. Averaging gives
  P = (1/3) * (all-ones 3x3 matrix), an S3-invariant projection with image W and kernel {x1 + x2 + x3 = 0}.
  Proof: P^2 = P because (J/3)^2 = J/3 for the all-ones matrix J; P commutes with permutation matrices; the rest is
  direct substitution. Certificate: certify.cert_maschke_example recomputes P from E exactly and checks all five facts.


5. SCHUR'S LEMMA

Theorem 2 (Schur) [T-2, proved_text]  Let V, W be irreducible and f: V -> W a G-homomorphism.
  (a) f = 0 or f is an isomorphism.
  (b) If V = W, then f = lambda * id for some lambda in C.
  Consequently dim Hom_G(V, W) = 1 if V and W are isomorphic and 0 otherwise.
Proof.
  (a) ker f is G-invariant in V (f(gv) = g f(v) = 0) and im f is G-invariant in W. By irreducibility ker f is 0 or V
      and im f is 0 or W. If f != 0 then ker f != V, so ker f = 0, and im f != 0, so im f = W: f is bijective.
  (b) By (LA1) f has an eigenvalue lambda. f - lambda*id is a G-homomorphism V -> V with nonzero kernel, so by (a) it is
      0.
  Last statement: if V, W are not isomorphic, (a) leaves only f = 0. If T: V -> W is an isomorphism, every
  f in Hom_G(V, W) gives T^-1 f in Hom_G(V, V) = C*id by (b), so f = lambda T, and Hom_G(V, W) = C*T has dimension 1.   QED

Corollary 1 [C-1, proved_text]  Every irreducible representation of an abelian group has degree 1.
Proof. For fixed g, rho(g) rho(h) = rho(gh) = rho(hg) = rho(h) rho(g), so rho(g) is a G-homomorphism V -> V and equals
  lambda_g * id by Theorem 2(b). Then every subspace is G-invariant; irreducibility forces dim V = 1.                 QED
Checked by: E-C1..E-C8 (all irreducibles of C_n have degree 1 and they exhaust the list).


6. AVERAGING AND THE FIRST ORTHOGONALITY RELATION

Lemma 2 (fixed points) [L-2, proved_text]  dim V^G = (1/|G|) sum_{g in G} chi_V(g).
Proof. Let P = (1/|G|) sum_g rho(g). For h in G, rho(h) P = (1/|G|) sum_g rho(hg) = P, so im P is in V^G. For v in
  V^G, Pv = (1/|G|) sum_g v = v. So P^2 = P and im P = V^G. By Lemma 1(a), dim V^G = tr P = (1/|G|) sum_g chi_V(g).   QED
Checked by: E-L2-S3, E-L2-D4, E-L2-A4, E-L2-S4 (transitive actions, 1 orbit) and E-L2-S3x (S3 on 5 points, 3 orbits).
  For a permutation representation dim V^G is the number of orbits (a vector is fixed iff it is constant on orbits);
  the certificate counts orbits combinatorially and compares with the character average.

Theorem 3 (orthogonality) [T-3, proved_text]  For representations V, W:  dim Hom_G(V, W) = <chi_W, chi_V>.
Proof. Apply Lemma 2 to the representation Hom(V, W) of D9. Its fixed points are Hom_G(V, W), and by Proposition 1(vi)
  its character is chi_W(g) conj(chi_V(g)). So dim Hom_G(V, W) = (1/|G|) sum_g chi_W(g) conj(chi_V(g)) = <chi_W, chi_V>.
  QED
Checked by: E-T3-S3, E-T3-S4: for all ordered pairs among the irreducibles plus the permutation representation of S3
  (16 pairs) and S4 (36 pairs), dim Hom_G(V, W) is computed by Gaussian elimination over Q (solving
  rho_W(s) X = X rho_V(s) for the generators s, without characters) and equals <chi_W, chi_V>.

Corollary 2 [C-2, proved_text]  Let V_1, ..., V_r be pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible representations with
  characters chi_1, ..., chi_r.
  (a) <chi_i, chi_j> = 1 if i = j and 0 otherwise (the irreducible characters are orthonormal).
  (b) If V is isomorphic to a direct sum with m_i copies of V_i (i = 1..r), then m_i = <chi_V, chi_i>. In particular the
      multiplicities do not depend on the chosen decomposition.
  (c) Two representations with the same character are isomorphic (given that all irreducible constituents are among
      the V_i).
  (d) <chi_V, chi_V> = sum m_i^2. V is irreducible if and only if <chi_V, chi_V> = 1.
  (e) Pairwise distinct characters with <chi, chi> = 1 and <chi, chi'> = 0 belong to pairwise non-isomorphic
      irreducible representations.
Proof.
  (a) Theorem 3 and Theorem 2. Also <chi_V, chi_W> = conj<chi_W, chi_V> = conj(dim Hom_G(W, V)), a real number, so the
      order of the arguments does not matter for characters.
  (b) chi_V = sum m_j chi_j by Proposition 1(iv),(v); take <., chi_i> and use (a).
  (c) By Theorem 1 both are direct sums of irreducibles; by (b) the multiplicities are read off from the character.
  (d) Expand with (a). If V is irreducible, V = V_1 for a suitable list, so <chi_V, chi_V> = 1. Conversely, by Theorem 1
      V is a direct sum of irreducibles; sum m_i^2 = 1 with nonnegative integers forces exactly one summand with m = 1.
  (e) By (d) each is irreducible. If two were isomorphic, Proposition 1(v) would give equal characters and then
      <chi, chi'> = <chi, chi> = 1, not 0.                                                                       QED


7. THE REGULAR REPRESENTATION AND COUNTING

Proposition 2 [P-2, proved_text]  Let chi_reg be the character of C[G].
  (a) chi_reg(e) = |G| and chi_reg(g) = 0 for g != e.
  (b) Every irreducible representation V occurs in C[G] with multiplicity dim V.
  (c) G has only finitely many irreducible representations up to isomorphism, V_1, ..., V_h, and
      sum_{i=1..h} (dim V_i)^2 = |G|.
  (d) Conversely, if V_1, ..., V_r are pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible and sum (dim V_i)^2 = |G|, the list is
      complete.
Proof.
  (a) rho(g) permutes the basis e_x; the diagonal entry at e_x is 1 iff gx = x iff g = e.
  (b) <chi_reg, chi_V> = (1/|G|) chi_reg(e) conj(chi_V(e)) = dim V by (a) and Proposition 1(i); apply Corollary 2(b)
      to a decomposition of C[G] (Theorem 1).
  (c) By (b) every irreducible occurs in the finite-dimensional C[G], so there are finitely many. Then C[G] is a direct
      sum of dim V_i copies of V_i for each i, and dim C[G] = |G| = sum (dim V_i)(dim V_i).
  (d) Any further irreducible V' would add (dim V')^2 >= 1 to the sum in (c).                                          QED
Checked by: every E-<group> certificate checks sum d_i^2 = |G| and chi_reg = sum_i d_i chi_i pointwise.

Theorem 4 [T-4, proved_text]  The irreducible characters chi_1, ..., chi_h form an orthonormal basis of the space of
  class functions. In particular h = number of conjugacy classes of G.
Proof. Orthonormality is Corollary 2(a); it remains to show they span. The space of class functions has the inner
  product of D7, so it suffices to show: a class function f with <f, chi_i> = 0 for all i is zero.
  For a representation (V, rho) put T = sum_g conj(f(g)) rho(g). For h in G,
     rho(h) T rho(h)^-1 = sum_g conj(f(g)) rho(hgh^-1) = sum_{g'} conj(f(h^-1 g' h)) rho(g') = T,
  using f(h^-1 g' h) = f(g'). So T is a G-homomorphism. If V = V_i is irreducible, T = lambda id by Theorem 2(b), and
     lambda dim V_i = tr T = sum_g conj(f(g)) chi_i(g) = |G| <chi_i, f> = |G| conj(<f, chi_i>) = 0.
  So T = 0 on every irreducible, hence on every representation (Theorem 1, block diagonal). On C[G]:
     0 = T e_e = sum_g conj(f(g)) e_g,
  and since the e_g are linearly independent, f(g) = 0 for all g.
  A class function is the same as a function on the set of conjugacy classes, so the space has dimension equal to the
  number of classes; an orthonormal basis has that many elements.                                                    QED
Checked by: every E-<group> certificate checks #irreducibles = #conjugacy classes (classes computed from the
  multiplication table).

Lemma 3 [L-3, proved_text]  If K is the conjugacy class of g, then |K| * |C_G(g)| = |G|.
Proof. hgh^-1 = h'gh'^-1 iff h^-1 h' commutes with g iff h' lies in the coset h C_G(g). So h -> hgh^-1 is constant on
  left cosets of C_G(g) and takes different values on different cosets; it maps the |G|/|C_G(g)| cosets (GT2)
  bijectively onto K.                                                                                                 QED

Theorem 5 (column orthogonality) [T-5, proved_text]  For g, g' in G:
  sum_{i=1..h} chi_i(g) conj(chi_i(g')) = |C_G(g)| if g and g' are conjugate, and 0 otherwise.
Proof. Let g_1, ..., g_h represent the h classes K_1, ..., K_h (Theorem 4: as many as irreducibles), and let X be the
  h x h matrix X_ij = chi_i(g_j) sqrt(|K_j|/|G|). Grouping the sum in <chi_i, chi_k> by classes,
     (X X*)_ik = sum_j (|K_j|/|G|) chi_i(g_j) conj(chi_k(g_j)) = <chi_i, chi_k> = delta_ik.
  So X X* = I. For a square matrix this implies X* X = I, i.e.
     sum_i conj(chi_i(g_j)) chi_i(g_l) sqrt(|K_j| |K_l|)/|G| = delta_jl.
  For j = l this gives sum_i |chi_i(g_j)|^2 = |G|/|K_j| = |C_G(g_j)| by Lemma 3; for j != l the sum is 0. Since
  characters are class functions, the statement holds for all g, g'.                                                   QED
Checked by: every E-<group> certificate checks all h^2 column sums exactly against |C_G(g)|.


8. CYCLIC GROUPS

Proposition 3 [P-3, proved_text]  Let G = C_n = <c>, zeta = exp(2 pi i/n). The irreducible representations of C_n are
  the n one-dimensional representations chi_j(c^k) = zeta^{jk}, j = 0, ..., n-1, pairwise non-isomorphic.
Proof. chi_j is a homomorphism since zeta^{j(k+l)} = zeta^{jk} zeta^{jl} and zeta^{jn} = 1 makes it well defined on
  exponents mod n. Degree-1 representations are irreducible. chi_j(c) = zeta^j are pairwise different, so the chi_j are
  pairwise distinct characters, hence pairwise non-isomorphic (Proposition 1(v)). n * 1^2 = n = |G|, so the list is
  complete (Proposition 2(d)). (Alternatively: by Corollary 1 every irreducible is a homomorphism chi: C_n -> C^*,
  determined by chi(c), which satisfies chi(c)^n = 1.)                                                               QED
Example E-C1 .. E-C8 [computed_rigorous]  For n = 1, ..., 8 the certificate checks the full chain of Section 9 below in
  the cyclotomic field Q(zeta_n).

10. A RESULT ON THE LIMITS OF CHARACTER TABLES

Proposition 4 [E-DQ, computed_rigorous + proved_text]  D4 and Q8 have the same character table (after matching classes
  of equal size and reordering irreducibles), but they are not isomorphic. So the character table does not determine
  the group.
Proof. The two tables in Examples E-D4 and E-Q8 coincide entry by entry with the column matching
  e<->1, r<->i, s<->j, r^2<->-1, rs<->k and equal class sizes 1, 2, 2, 1, 2. An isomorphism preserves element orders.
  D4 has 5 elements of order 2 (r^2 and the four reflections s, rs, r^2 s, r^3 s); Q8 has exactly one (-1), since
  i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = -1 makes the other six elements of order 4.                                                        QED
Certificate: certify.cert_same_table_not_isomorphic searches all class bijections respecting class sizes for one under
  which the two tables agree as sets of rows (found), and counts elements of order 2 from the multiplication tables
  (5 and 1). Contrast in the self-test: C4 and C2 x C2 do NOT have equal tables (C4 has the non-real value i).

R1  Complete reducibility (Theorem 1) and Schur's lemma (Theorem 2), with full proofs.
