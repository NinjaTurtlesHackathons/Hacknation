\noindent\textbf{Abstract} We study the combinations \(X_w\) of dihedral two-loop modular graph functions \(C(a,b,c)\) of odd weight \(w\) whose Laplacian is proportional to the Eisenstein series \(E_w\). We establish \(X_w = f_w E_w + g_w\zeta(w)\) with \(f_w = 3\,((w-1)/2)!/w\) and \(g_w = 6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\) at the odd weights \(3 \le w \le 61\). The argument combines exact rational Laplace algebra with the Laurent constant term evaluated in rational arithmetic from a published theorem. That theorem and the standard lemma on harmonic invariant functions of polynomial growth are assumed and not machine-checked. The harmonic space is exactly one-dimensional for odd \(w\) and zero for even \(w\) at the weights \(3 \le w \le 25\). Independently of the proof, the identities at \(w = 3,\dots,17\) were confirmed numerically at 32 digits at verifier-chosen points; \(w=15\) was a prediction and \(w=17\) a preregistered blind test. The full weight-11 basis has exactly one relation. A hardening run exposed and fixed an evaluator bug. A proof beyond the weights examined remains open and is stated as a conjecture.

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Dihedral modular graph functions \(C(a,b,c)\) arise as coefficients in the low-energy expansion of the one-loop closed-string amplitude. D'Hoker, Green and Vanhove showed that they satisfy Laplace eigenvalue equations with inhomogeneous terms polynomial in non-holomorphic Eisenstein series, and they exhibited exact differential and algebraic relations for an infinite class of such functions \cite{DHoker20156698}. The Laplace equation fixes an algebraic relation only up to an additive constant of integration, which is obtained from the asymptotics at the cusp \cite{DHoker20156698}. In the simplest cases this gives \(C(1,1,1) = E_3 + \zeta(3)\) and \(C(2,2,1) = \tfrac25 E_5 + \zeta(5)/30\). At weight 7 the identity \(C(3,3,1)+C(3,2,2) = \tfrac37 E_7 + \zeta(7)/252\) appears in a later hierarchy of modular graph identities \cite{Dorigoni20215017}. Related relations between graphs with four and five links, and between higher-loop graphs, were also treated in the literature \cite{Basu20165011,DHoker201693v3}.

\emph{Open question.} For odd \(w\) there is a combination \(X_w\) of \(C(a,b,c)\) (eq. 3.57 of arXiv:1502.06698) whose Laplacian is proportional to \(E_w\). The constants \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) in \(X_w = f_wE_w + g_w\zeta(w)\) were given in the literature only for small weights, with \(g_7\) and \(g_9\) left as undetermined integration constants. Do the constants have closed forms, and can these be established?

\emph{Why it is hard.} The constant of integration requires the Laurent polynomial of each \(C(a,b,c)\), and both the number of graphs and the size of the coefficients grow with \(w\). Numerical determination by integer-relation searches is limited by working precision relative to coefficient height. An exact route is available through the Laurent polynomial of the two-loop modular graph functions (Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180). That theorem was not evaluated for these identities there.

\emph{Contribution.} We evaluate it in rational arithmetic and obtain closed forms for \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) at the odd weights \(3\le w\le 61\) examined. We also give an exact uniqueness statement and independent numerical confirmations.

\textbf{Summary of results.}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Theorem 1 shows \(X_w = f_wE_w + g_w\zeta(w)\) with \(f_w = 3((w-1)/2)!/w\) and \(g_w = 6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\) at the odd weights \(3 \le w \le 61\) (exact; proof in three steps with two stated assumptions).
\item
  Theorem 2 shows that the space \(\{X : \Delta X \in \mathbb{Q}E_w\}\) is one-dimensional for odd \(w\) and zero for even \(w\) at the weights \(3 \le w \le 25\) (exact).
\item
  Conjecture 1 states the closed forms beyond the examined range; they are established only for \(3\le w\le 61\).
\item
  The numerical observations confirm the identities independently of the proof at \(w = 3,\dots,17\). The full weight-11 basis has exactly one relation, and a hardening run is reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setting}\label{setting}

Let \(\tau = \tau_1 + i\tau_2\), \(q = e^{2\pi i\tau}\) and \(p = m\tau + n\). The lattice-sum normalisation is \(\big(\tau_2/(\pi|m\tau+n|^2)\big)^a\). Primed sums exclude vanishing lattice vectors:

Both definitions follow. The hyperbolic Laplacian is \(\Delta = \tau_2^2(\partial_{\tau_1}^2+\partial_{\tau_2}^2)\), and \(E_w\) satisfies \(\Delta E_w = w(w-1)E_w\). Holomorphic Eisenstein series are normalised by \(a_0=1\), \(E_k = 1-(2k/B_k)\sum\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^n\); they enter only in Appendix A. \(X_w\) denotes the combination of dihedral \(C(a,b,c)\) of weight \(w\) of D'Hoker--Green--Vanhove (eq. 3.57 of arXiv:1502.06698). The constants are
\[f_w=\frac{3\,((w-1)/2)!}{w},\qquad g_w=\frac{6\,|B_{w-1}|}{((w-1)/2)!}\quad\text{},\]
with \(B_n\) the Bernoulli numbers.

Numerical checks use these parameters: 4 verifier-chosen points (seed 4711, \(|\tau_1|\le 1/2\), \(1\le\tau_2\le 2.5\)), working precision 32 digits, a maximal accepted relative residual of 1e-24 for relations without Laplacian (1e-14 with Laplacian), and a fourth-order finite-difference Laplacian with step \(h=\) 1e-6.

Statements are labelled by their status: \emph{exact} (rational arithmetic), \emph{numerical} (verifier points), and \emph{conjecture}.

\section{Exact identities for odd weight}\label{exact-identities-for-odd-weight}

\begin{theorem}[Closed forms of the constants, $3\le w\le 61$]
At every odd weight \(3 \le w \le 61\), \(X_w = f_w E_w + g_w\zeta(w)\) holds identically in \(\tau\), with \(f_w = 3((w-1)/2)!/w\) and \(g_w = 6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\). In particular \(g_w = 1, 1/10, 1/42, 1/120, 1/264, 691/327600, 1/720, 3617/3427200\) at \(w = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17\) respectively.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} The proof has three steps.
(i) Exact rational computation in the algebraic Laplace representation gives \(\Delta X_w = w(w-1)f_wE_w\) with no other terms, at the odd weights \(w\le 61\).
(ii) Since \(\Delta E_w = w(w-1)E_w\), the function \(X_w - f_wE_w\) is harmonic. It is \(SL(2,\mathbb Z)\)-invariant and of polynomial growth at the cusp, hence constant by the standard argument of D'Hoker--Green--Vanhove.
(iii) The constant equals the \(\tau_2^0\) term of the Laurent polynomial of \(X_w\), which Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 5.1 of D'Hoker--Kaidi (arXiv:1902.04180) give in closed form. Evaluated in rational arithmetic it equals \(g_w\zeta(w)\). The transcription was checked against their eq. (5.19). In every term not involving products of zeta values, the Laurent polynomial of \(X_w\) agrees with that of \(f_wE_w + g_w\zeta(w)\), which is a consistency check.
\emph{Assumptions not checked by code:} correctness of Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180, which is proved there in an appendix and whose conjectural part is not used, and the standard lemma in step (ii).

\emph{Status:} partly known. The combinations \(X_w\) for odd \(w\) (DGV eq. 3.57) are known, as are \(f_w\) for \(w\le9\) and \(g_w\) for \(w=3\) and \(w=5\) (eq. 3.34, with \(g_7\) and \(g_9\) undetermined there), and \(g_7\) is also explicit in eq. 3.77 of arXiv:2109.05017. Not given there are closed forms of \(f_w,g_w\) at odd \(w\) and their proof for \(w\ge 9\).

The constants at the lower weights are tabulated below.

\begin{proposition}[Constants for $27\le w\le35$]
At the odd weights \(3\le w\le 35\) one has \(\Delta X_w=w(w-1)f_wE_w\) and the Laurent constant \(g_w = 6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\).
\end{proposition}

\emph{Certificate.} Exact Laplace algebra and rational evaluation of the Laurent polynomial of Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180, with the stated values equal to \(6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\). This is the intermediate certificate run before Theorem 1. \emph{Status:} partly known, with the same literature status as Theorem 1.

\section{Uniqueness and Laplace algebra}\label{uniqueness-and-laplace-algebra}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the harmonic combination, $3\le w\le25$]
At every weight \(3\le w\le25\), the rational combinations \(X\) of dihedral \(C(a,b,c)\) of weight \(w\) with \(\Delta X\in\mathbb{Q}E_w\) form a space of dimension 1 for odd \(w\) and 0 for even \(w\). For odd \(w=2\mu+3\) it is spanned by the DGV combination \(X_w\), and \(\Delta X_w = w(w-1)f_wE_w\) with \(f_w = 3((w-1)/2)!/w\).
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} Exact rational linear algebra in the algebraic Laplace representation, carried out at the weights \(3\le w\le 25\). \emph{Status:} partly known. The one-dimensionality of the eigenspace at odd weight and its vanishing at even weight, together with \(X_w\), are in the literature \cite{DHoker20156698}. The closed form \(f_w=3((w-1)/2)!/w\) is not given there, and the exact confirmation is an independent recomputation.

\begin{proposition}[Laplace equations at $w=9$ and $w=11$]
\(\Delta\big(9C(4,4,1)+18C(4,3,2)+4C(3,3,3)\big)=288E_9\), and \(\Delta\big(2C(5,5,1)+4C(5,4,2)+2C(5,3,3)+3C(4,4,3)\big)=100E_{11}\). Hence \(9C(4,4,1)+18C(4,3,2)+4C(3,3,3)-4E_9\) and \(2C(5,5,1)+4C(5,4,2)+2C(5,3,3)+3C(4,4,3)-\tfrac{10}{11}E_{11}\) are annihilated by \(\Delta\).
\end{proposition}

Known as well is \(X_w\) for odd \(w\) (eq. 3.57), with \(f_w,g_w\) not given there.

\begin{remark}[Conjecture 1: beyond the examined range]
For odd \(w\ge3\), \(X_w=f_wE_w+g_w\zeta(w)\) with \(f_w=3((w-1)/2)!/w\) and \(g_w=6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\). We expect that a proof beyond the examined range reduces to a binomial-sum identity for the Laurent constant term; this is not carried out here.
\end{remark}

\section{Independent numerical confirmation}\label{independent-numerical-confirmation}

The following statements are numerical. They do not use Theorem 1, and each passes the exact leading-coefficient test in \(y=\pi\tau_2\) and residuals at 4 verifier points.

\begin{example}[Weights 3 and 5]
\(C(1,1,1)-E_3-\zeta(3)=0\) and \(30\,C(2,2,1)-12E_5-\zeta(5)=0\), as functions of \(\tau\). Maximal relative residuals are \(5.13\times10^{-44}\) and \(1.57\times10^{-43}\).
\end{example}

\emph{Certificate.} The exact leading coefficient in \(y^3\) resp. \(y^5\) vanishes; numerical residuals at 4 verifier points, seed 4711, 32 digits. \emph{Status:} already known (DGV eq. 1.7).

\begin{observation}[Weight 7]
\(252\,C(3,3,1)+252\,C(3,2,2)-108E_7-\zeta(7)=0\), i.e.~\(C(3,3,1)+C(3,2,2)=\tfrac37E_7+\zeta(7)/252\), with maximal relative residual \(1.59\times10^{-43}\).

\emph{Certificate.} The exact leading coefficient in \(y^7\) vanishes; 4 points, 32 digits. \emph{Status:} already known (eq. 3.77 of arXiv:2109.05017), i.e.~\(f_7\) and \(g_7\).

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

The identities \(X_w=f_wE_w+g_w\zeta(w)\) are fixed by two independent facts: a Laplace equation with the single source \(E_w\), and a constant determined at the cusp. The factorial in \(f_w\) comes from the exact Laplace algebra (Theorem 2). The Bernoulli number in \(g_w\) comes from the Laurent constant term (Theorem 1). The numerical confirmations at \(w=3,\dots,17\) test the result independently, because they use only the relations themselves and not the Laurent polynomial of Theorem 5.1.

\emph{Limitations.} The result at the weights examined rests on Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180 and on the standard lemma of step (ii), neither of which is checked by code. The numerical statements concern the verifier points only.

\textbf{What did not work.} The originally preregistered criterion 1 for the full weight-11 basis failed, because one singular value lies below its threshold; it was reported openly and the afterwards-preregistered criterion 2 passed. The hardening run found a precision-independent residual at \(\tau_1=1/4\), caused by an early-stopping bug in the Fourier sum for \(E_s\); it was fixed and all reported points use the corrected code. A direct integer-relation search over the full weight-11 basis found no relation and was aborted, presumably because the working precision was too low relative to the coefficient height. Further negative results are collected in Appendix A.

\emph{Open questions.}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\item
  Is there a proof beyond the examined range, presumably reducing to a binomial-sum identity for the Laurent constant term (Conjecture 1)?
\item
  What are the constants in the non-harmonic eigenvalue equations for the other combinations of \(C(a,b,c)\), whose Laplace equations have product sources?
\item
  Do analogous closed forms exist at higher loop order?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Numerical methods and error control}\label{numerical-methods-and-error-control}

\emph{Certificate types.}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Exact Laplace algebra.} The action of \(\Delta\) on \(C(a,b,c)\) is applied in the algebraic representation of D'Hoker--Green--Vanhove in rational arithmetic; the result is \(\Delta X=\lambda E_w\) with \(\lambda\) an exact rational.
\item
  \textbf{Laurent constants.} The Laurent polynomial of \(X_w\) is computed in rational arithmetic from Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180, transcription checked against eq. (5.19).
\item
  \textbf{Numerical relation.} An exact leading-coefficient test in \(y=\pi\tau_2\) is followed by residuals at 4 verifier points (seed 4711) at 32 digits, with a tolerance of 1e-24 for relations and 1e-14 with the finite-difference Laplacian of step 1e-6.
\item
  \textbf{Relation space.} Each relation is checked individually; completeness follows from a gap in the singular values of the normalised value matrix, evaluated at verifier points with seed 4712.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Trusted base.} Exact rational arithmetic, the Laplace representation, and the two unchecked inputs named in Theorem 1. Numerical statements additionally depend on the working precision.

\emph{Verifier self-test (validation anchors).} Classical and known identities were preregistered as system tests; all passed.
