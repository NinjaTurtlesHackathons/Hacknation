\begin{abstract}
We study rational linear relations among two-loop dihedral modular graph functions \(C(a,b,c)\) of weight \(w=a+b+c\), together with \(E(w)\) and \(\zeta(w)\). For the full dihedral basis at weights 3, 5, 7 and 9 the relation space is numerically of dimension exactly one per weight. At weight 9 it is spanned by \(960E(9)+\zeta(9)-2160C(4,4,1)-4320C(4,3,2)-960C(3,3,3)=0\). At weight 11 the relation space was determined only on the sub-basis \(C(5,5,1),C(5,4,2),C(5,3,3),C(4,4,3),E(11),\zeta(11)\), where it is again one-dimensional. For the full ten-function weight-11 basis the question is undecided. A counting of linearly independent modular graph forms was given in \cite{Gerken202056v2}, and the weight-9 result may be implied there. We also verify inhomogeneous Laplace equations for \(C(3,1,1)\), \(C(2,1,1)\) and \(C(2,2,1)\). The method is PSLQ on values at random points, followed by an exact leading-Laurent-coefficient check and independent evaluation. All modular-graph-function statements are numerical observations at 4 verifier points, not theorems.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} modular graph functions, Eisenstein series, string theory one-loop amplitude, Laplace equation, linear relations, low-energy expansion
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Modular graph functions appear as coefficients in the low-energy expansion of the one-loop closed-string amplitude. They satisfy Laplace eigenvalue equations with inhomogeneous terms that are polynomial in non-holomorphic Eisenstein series \cite{DHoker20156698}. Relations of low weight have been proved by an explicit map between graph forms \cite{DHoker20160839}, and all dihedral identities at weight seven are in the literature \cite{DHoker201693v3}. Systematic basis decompositions of two- and three-point graph forms are available \cite{Gerken202076v2}. A counting of linearly independent modular graph forms gives predictions for higher, hitherto unexplored weights \cite{Gerken202056v2}.

Open question: how many independent rational linear relations exist among all \(C(a,b,c)\) of weight \(w\), \(E(w)\) and \(\zeta(w)\) for weights 3, 5, 7, 9 and 11, and which Laplace equations do the functions satisfy?

Contribution: we determine the relation space numerically on the full dihedral basis at weights 3, 5, 7 and 9. At weight 11 we determine it only on a six-function sub-basis. We verify Laplace equations for three functions, and we reproduce holomorphic identities with Sturm-bound certificates.

\textbf{Summary of results.}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  The weight-by-weight observations show that the relation space has dimension exactly one on the full dihedral basis at weights 3, 5, 7 and 9, with the novelty statuses quoted there.
\item
  A further observation gives the explicit weight-9 relation.
\item
  The weight-11 observation gives the relation and its one-dimensional relation space on the sub-basis only. The full ten-function weight-11 basis is undecided.
\item
  The Laplace observations give equations for \(C(3,1,1)\), \(C(2,1,1)\) and \(C(2,2,1)\).
\item
  The propositions on holomorphic identities certify string-partition-function identities, and a further proposition gives the eta-quotient span table.
\end{itemize}

\section{Model and assumptions}\label{model-and-assumptions}

\textbf{Holomorphic objects.} \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\) is the expansion variable. The Eisenstein series are normalised by \(E_k=1-(2k/B_k)\sum_n\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^n\), so that \(a_0=1\). Further building blocks are \(\eta(d)=\eta(d\tau)\) (Dedekind eta), \(\Delta(d)\) (the discriminant form at \(d\tau\)), \(\theta(d)=\sum_n q^{dn^2}\), the lattice theta series \(\theta_{E8}\) and \(\theta_{D16}\) obtained by lattice enumeration, and \(j=E_4^3/\Delta\).

\textbf{Non-holomorphic objects.} \(\tau=\tau_1+i\tau_2\) and \(p=m\tau+n\). The lattice sums are normalised with weight factor \((\tau_2/(\pi|m\tau+n|^2))^a\):

with weight \(w=a+b+c\). \(\mathcal L=\tau_2^2(\partial_{\tau_1}^2+\partial_{\tau_2}^2)\) is the hyperbolic Laplacian, and \(\zeta\) is the Riemann zeta function.

\section{Method}\label{method}

\textbf{Relations.} Candidate rational relations are obtained by PSLQ on function values at random points. The failed weight-11 attempt on the full basis, at the points examined (6-8 points, up to 64 digits), found no relation. Each candidate is then checked in two ways:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  exact rational arithmetic on the leading Laurent coefficient;
\item
  evaluation at the verifier's own points, with relative residual below \texttt{1e-24}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Completeness.} Completeness of a relation space is judged by a singular-value gap of the normalised value matrix at an independent seed, 4712.

\textbf{Laplace equations.} They are checked at the 4 verifier points at 32 digits, with relative residual at most \texttt{1e-14}, using a finite-difference Laplacian, together with an exact leading-coefficient check.

\textbf{Holomorphic identities.} A Sturm-bound certificate is used. For the identity of, the terms lie in \(M^!_2(\Gamma_0(4))\) and the coefficients \(q^0..q^{11}\) vanish exactly, which exceeds the Sturm bound 1.

\textbf{Trusted base and status.} The trusted base is exact rational arithmetic for leading coefficients and Sturm certificates, plus the verifier's 32-digit evaluation of modular graph functions. Everything about modular graph functions beyond the exact leading coefficient is a numerical observation at the 4 verifier points, never a theorem. The weight-11 sub-basis came from a human-guided exploration and was then verified by the code verifier. Everything is re-run with the command in the Declarations.

The work was carried out by an automated, verifier-gated laboratory in which every statement reported here was accepted only after an independent code check; its organisation is described in Appendix A.

\subsection{Holomorphic identities from string partition functions}\label{holomorphic-identities-from-string-partition-functions}

These identities are the holomorphic building blocks of string partition functions: the Jacobi identity as an eta-quotient identity, the two heterotic lattices, and the discriminant. Each is certified exactly by a Sturm bound. They are certified statements, not numerical observations.

\begin{proposition}[Jacobi identity on $\Gamma_0(4)$]
\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  \(\theta(1)^4-\eta(2)^{20}\eta(1)^{-8}\eta(4)^{-8}=0\) (weight 2).
\item
  \(\theta(1)^2-\eta(2)^{10}\eta(1)^{-4}\eta(4)^{-4}=0\) (weight 1).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Heterotic lattices on $\Gamma_0(1)$]
\(\theta_{E8}-E_4=0\); \(\theta_{E8}^2-\theta_{D16}=0\); \(\theta_{E8}^2-E_4^2=0\); \(\theta_{E8}^2-E_8=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Discriminant and $j$ on $\Gamma_0(1)$]
\(1728\,\Delta(1)-E_4^3+E_6^2=0\), and \(j\,\Delta(1)-E_4^3=0\).
\end{proposition}

\emph{Certificate.} In each case all terms lie in \(M^!_k(\Gamma_0(N))\) of equal weight, and the coefficients vanish exactly up to the stated order, which exceeds the Sturm bound (Table 3).

\section{Negative results}\label{negative-results}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Weight 6 (undecided).} The question concerned relations among \(C(4,1,1),C(3,2,1),C(2,2,2),E(6),E(2)E(4),E(3)^2,E(2)^3,\zeta(3)E(3),\zeta(3)^2\). No claim passed the verifier. The value matrix (12x9) has no singular value below \texttt{1e-24} and a smallest large singular value of 2.44e-8. The singular-value gap criterion is therefore not met, and the dimension remains undecided.
\item
  \textbf{Weight 11, first attempt with the full basis.} PSLQ over the full 12-element basis (ten \(C(a,b,c)\), \(E(11)\), \(\zeta(11)\)), at the points examined (6-8 points, up to 64 digits), found no relation. The run was aborted. The suspected cause is precision that is too low relative to the coefficient height. This is why the weight-11 result is restricted to the sub-basis.
\item
  \textbf{First eta-span run.} No claim passed the verifier. The failure came from a verifier capacity limit, which was fixed, and the table above was then obtained.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion, limitations and open questions}\label{discussion-limitations-and-open-questions}

The relation space has dimension exactly one at weights 3, 5, 7 and 9 on the full dihedral basis. Each relation has the form (combination of \(C\)) \(=\) (rational) \(E(w)+\) (rational) \(\zeta(w)\). The weight-11 relation has the same shape on a six-function sub-basis. All statements on modular graph functions are numerical observations at 4 verifier points, with an exact check only of the leading Laurent coefficient.

Open questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\item
  What is the relation-space dimension for the full ten-function weight-11 basis? Higher precision or a different method is needed.
\item
  What is the dimension at weight 6, where the gap criterion was not met?
\item
  Is the weight-9 result implied by the counting of \cite{Gerken202056v2} (status: inconclusive)?
\item
  Can the numerical relations be turned into proofs, as done for low weight in \cite{DHoker20160839} \cite{DHoker201693v3}?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Agentic laboratory}\label{agentic-laboratory}

The laboratory ran 8 rounds with 24 verified statements and 3 negative results. Every round was preregistered before the experiment (\texttt{prereg.md}). Several agents with distinct roles proposed candidates (for example numerical, skeptical and theoretical roles, as listed in the failed attempts). Only statements accepted by the code verifier were kept. Counter-checks (adversarial tests) numbered 30 in total: 2 passed, 27 did not pass, and 1 was not executable. ``Did not pass'' means the false variant was rejected, which is the intended outcome. The two that passed did not contradict the statements.

\section{Verifier self-test}\label{verifier-self-test}

The verifier is tested with deliberately false variants, which it must reject. Examples:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  factor 1727 instead of 1728 in the discriminant identity, and 1729 instead of 1728;
\item
  \(q^1\)-coefficient 481 instead of 480 for \(\theta_{D16}\);
\item
  \(\zeta(5)\) coefficient \(1/5\) instead of \(1/10\), which gives residual 1.04e-01;
\item
  \(\zeta(9)\) coefficient 2 instead of 1;
\item
  a sign flip of the \(\zeta(11)\) term, which gives residual 5.71e-01.
\end{itemize}

All of these were rejected, so the verifier distinguishes exact leading coefficients and tight residuals from near-misses.
