\noindent\textbf{Abstract} We consider the combinations \(X_w\) of two-loop dihedral modular graph functions \(C(a,b,c)\) of odd weight \(w\) whose hyperbolic Laplacian is a multiple of the Eisenstein series \(E(w)\). At each odd weight examined, \(3\le w\le 61\), we prove \(X_w=f_wE(w)+g_w\zeta(w)\) with \(f_w=3((w-1)/2)!/w\) and \(g_w=6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\). The proof combines exact Laplace algebra, a harmonicity, invariance and growth argument, and a constant term evaluated from a Laurent polynomial in rational arithmetic. It assumes a theorem of D'Hoker--Kaidi and a standard lemma, neither checked by code. We also show exactly that at each weight \(3\le w\le 25\) the space of combinations with \(\Delta X\in\mathbb{Q}E(w)\) has dimension 1 for odd \(w\) and 0 for even \(w\). Independently of the proof, the constants at \(w=3,\dots,17\) are confirmed numerically at 32 digits at verifier-chosen points, and \(w=17\) was a preregistered blind test. The full weight-11 basis has exactly one relation, established numerically by a singular-value criterion. A proof beyond the examined range remains open.
\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords} Modular graph functions $\cdot$ Laplace eigenvalue equations $\cdot$ Eisenstein series $\cdot$ Bernoulli numbers $\cdot$ Genus-one string amplitudes $\cdot$ Computer-assisted proof\par\medskip
\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Two-loop dihedral modular graph functions \(C(a,b,c)\) appear as coefficients in the low-energy expansion of the one-loop amplitude of closed strings. D'Hoker, Green and Vanhove showed that they satisfy Laplace eigenvalue equations with inhomogeneous terms polynomial in non-holomorphic Eisenstein series, and that the Laplace equation fixes an algebraic identity only up to an additive constant of integration \cite{DHoker20156698}. The DGV combination \(X_w\) of \(C(a,b,c)\), whose Laplacian is proportional to \(E(w)\), is used here at each odd weight examined, \(3\le w\le 61\). The constants are explicit in that work only up to weight 9, and \(g_7,g_9\) appear there as undetermined integration constants. The constant \(g_7\) was later given in a general procedure based on Laurent polynomials \cite{Dorigoni20215017}. Identities among two-loop graphs at all weights were also constructed earlier \cite{DHoker201693v3}, and relations between graphs with four and five links have been proved \cite{Basu20165011}.

\textbf{Open question.} What are the closed forms of \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) in \(X_w=f_wE(w)+g_w\zeta(w)\) for odd \(w\)?

\textbf{Difficulty.} The Laplace equation alone leaves \(g_w\) undetermined \cite{DHoker20156698}. Fixing it requires the Laurent polynomial of each \(C(a,b,c)\) at the cusp. The size of the combination grows with \(w\), and numerical searches for relations are limited by the working precision relative to the height of the coefficients.

\textbf{Contribution.} The Laurent polynomial of each single \(C_{u,v;w}\) is known (Theorem 5.1 of D'Hoker--Kaidi, arXiv:1902.04180). We evaluate it in rational arithmetic on the DGV combinations. This gives closed forms for \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) with a proof at each odd weight examined, \(w\le 61\), together with numerical confirmations independent of that proof.

\textbf{Summary of results.}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Theorem 1 shows \(X_w=f_wE(w)+g_w\zeta(w)\) exactly at each odd weight examined, \(3\le w\le 61\), with \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) in closed form. The proof is computer-assisted in rational arithmetic, and its assumptions are stated openly.
\item
  Theorem 2 shows that the space \(\{X:\Delta X\in\mathbb{Q}E(w)\}\) is one-dimensional for odd \(w\) and zero for even \(w\), at each weight \(3\le w\le 25\).
\item
  Conjecture 1 states that the closed forms persist beyond the examined range; they are proved up to \(w=61\).
\item
  Observations 1 to 3 confirm numerically, independently of the proof, the constants at \(w=3,\dots,17\). They show that the full weight-11 basis carries exactly one relation. They also report a hardening run that exposed and fixed one evaluator bug.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setting}\label{setting}

Let \(\tau=\tau_1+i\tau_2\) and \(p=m\tau+n\) with \((m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^2\setminus\{0\}\). The non-holomorphic Eisenstein series and the dihedral two-loop graphs are

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle E(s)=\sum_{p}{}' \Big(\frac{\tau_2}{\pi|p|^2}\Big)^{s},$}\]

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle C(a,b,c)=\sum_{p_1+p_2+p_3=0}{}' \prod_{i=1}^{3}\Big(\frac{\tau_2}{\pi|p_i|^2}\Big)^{a_i},$}\]

with \((a_1,a_2,a_3)=(a,b,c)\) and weight \(w=a+b+c\). The hyperbolic Laplacian is

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle \Delta=\tau_2^2\big(\partial_{\tau_1}^2+\partial_{\tau_2}^2\big),$}\]

and \(E(w)\) satisfies

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle \Delta E(w)=w(w-1)E(w).$}\]

\(\zeta\) is the Riemann zeta function and \(B_k\) are Bernoulli numbers. For odd \(w=2\mu+3\), \(X_w\) denotes the rational combination of \(C(a,b,c)\) of weight \(w\) given by eq. 3.57 of D'Hoker--Green--Vanhove (arXiv:1502.06698), in that normalisation. The constants are

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle f_w=\frac{3\,((w-1)/2)!}{w},$}\]

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle g_w=\frac{6\,|B_{w-1}|}{((w-1)/2)!}.$}\]

Holomorphic Eisenstein series are normalised by \(a_0=1\), \(E_k=1-\frac{2k}{B_k}\sum_n\sigma_{k-1}(n)q^n\) with \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\).

\textbf{Verifier parameters.} The numerical verifier uses 4 evaluation points with seed 4711 in the region \(|\tau_1|\le 1/2\), \(1\le\tau_2\le 2.5\). The working precision is 32 digits. Numerical relations are accepted at maximal relative residual 1e-24, and at 1e-14 when a Laplacian is involved. The Laplacian is evaluated by a fourth-order finite-difference scheme with step h = 1e-6.

\section{Closed forms and their proof}\label{closed-forms-and-their-proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed forms for odd weight]
At each odd weight \(w\) examined, \(3\le w\le 61\), one has \(X_w=f_wE(w)+g_w\zeta(w)\), with the constants

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle f_w=\frac{3((w-1)/2)!}{w}\quad\text{}$}\]

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle g_w=\frac{6|B_{w-1}|}{((w-1)/2)!}\quad\text{}$}\]
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} The proof has three steps, following.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Exact rational computation in the algebraic Laplace representation gives \(\Delta X_w=w(w-1)f_wE(w)\) with no other terms, re-done at each odd weight examined, \(w\le 61\). Since \(\Delta E(w)=w(w-1)E(w)\), the difference \(X_w-f_wE(w)\) is harmonic.
\item
  The difference is \(SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\)-invariant and of polynomial growth at the cusp, hence constant by the standard argument used by D'Hoker--Green--Vanhove.
\item
  The constant equals the \(\tau_2^0\) term of the Laurent polynomial of \(X_w\). This polynomial follows from Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 5.1 of D'Hoker--Kaidi (arXiv:1902.04180), evaluated in rational arithmetic. The transcription was checked against their eq. 5.19, and every Laurent term not involving products of zeta values agrees with that of \(f_wE(w)+g_w\zeta(w)\). This is a consistency check. The \(\tau_2^0\) term equals \(g_w\zeta(w)\) with \(g_w=6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\) at each odd weight examined, \(w\le 61\).
\end{enumerate}

Assumptions not checked by code: the correctness of Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180, proved there in Appendix A, whose conjectural part concerns only the coefficient of \(\tau_2^{2-w}\) and is not used, and the standard lemma in step 2. \(\square\)

\emph{Novelty status:} partly known. The combinations \(X_w\) (DGV eq. 3.57), \(f_w\) up to weight 9 and \(g_w\) at weights 3 and 5 (DGV eq. 3.34) are due to DGV, and \(g_7\) also appears in arXiv:2109.05017. Not given there are the closed forms for general odd \(w\) and their proof for \(w\ge 9\). The Laurent polynomial of each single \(C_{u,v;w}\) is used here as a tool.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the harmonic combination]
At each weight \(w\) examined, \(3\le w\le 25\), the rational combinations \(X\) of dihedral \(C(a,b,c)\) of weight \(w\) with \(\Delta X\in\mathbb{Q}\,E(w)\) form a vector space of dimension 1 for odd \(w\) and 0 for even \(w\). For odd \(w\) it is spanned by the DGV combination \(X_w\), and \(\Delta X_w=w(w-1)f_wE(w)\).
\end{theorem}

\emph{Certificate.} The algebraic Laplace representation of D'Hoker--Green--Vanhove was re-derived and the linear conditions were solved in exact rational arithmetic for each \(w\) in the range. \(\square\)

\emph{Novelty status:} partly known. DGV state that the eigenvalue-0 eigenspace has multiplicity one for each odd weight and zero for each even weight; the closed form \(f_w=3((w-1)/2)!/w\) is not given there, and the exact confirmation up to \(w=25\) is an independent recomputation.

\begin{remark}[Conjecture 1]
The closed forms \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) of Theorem 1 are conjectured to persist at odd weights beyond the examined range. Before the proof, the formula was a conjecture read off from \(w=3..13\). It is now proved up to \(w=61\). A proof beyond that range is open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Status of the computer-assisted parts]
The trusted base consists of exact rational arithmetic in the Laplace algebra and in the Laurent polynomials of Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180, plus the two assumptions listed in the proof of Theorem 1. In the numerical observations the base is 32-digit arithmetic at verifier-chosen points, together with an exact check of the leading Laurent coefficient. A third party re-runs every certificate with `python -m asd.recheck modular' from the repository given in Appendix A.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact Laplace computations at weights 9 and 11}\label{exact-laplace-computations-at-weights-9-and-11}

\begin{proposition}[Weight 9]
Exactly, in rational arithmetic,

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle \Delta\big(9C(4,4,1)+18C(4,3,2)+4C(3,3,3)\big)=288\,E(9)\quad\text{}$}\]

so \(9C(4,4,1)+18C(4,3,2)+4C(3,3,3)-4E(9)\) is annihilated by \(\Delta\).
\end{proposition}

\emph{Certificate.} The algebraic Laplace representation of DGV was re-derived and checked in rational arithmetic; the verifier returns \(\lambda=288\). \(\square\)

\emph{Novelty status:} already known for weights 3, 5, 7 and 9 (DGV eq. 3.33).

\begin{proposition}[Weight 11]
Exactly, in rational arithmetic,

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle \Delta\big(2C(5,5,1)+4C(5,4,2)+2C(5,3,3)+3C(4,4,3)\big)=100\,E(11)\quad\text{}$}\]

so \(2C(5,5,1)+4C(5,4,2)+2C(5,3,3)+3C(4,4,3)-\tfrac{10}{11}E(11)\) is annihilated by \(\Delta\).
\end{proposition}

\emph{Certificate.} As for the weight-9 proposition; the verifier returns \(\lambda=100\). \(\square\)

\emph{Novelty status:} the combination for each odd \(w\) is already known (DGV eq. 3.57); \(f_w\) and \(g_w\) are not given there.

\section{Numerical confirmation, independent of the proof}\label{numerical-confirmation-independent-of-the-proof}

The following observations are numerical. Each relation was checked at 4 verifier-chosen points (seed 4711, 32 digits). The leading Laurent coefficient was checked exactly and vanishes in every case.

\begin{observation}[Relations at $w=3,\dots,17$]
As functions of \(\tau\), at relative residual below 1e-24:

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle C(1,1,1)-E(3)-\zeta(3)=0\quad\text{}$}\]

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle 30\,C(2,2,1)-12\,E(5)-\zeta(5)=0\quad\text{}$}\]

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle 252\,C(3,3,1)+252\,C(3,2,2)=108\,E(7)+\zeta(7)\quad\text{}$}\]

At weight 9,

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle \begin{aligned}&-2160\,C(4,4,1)-4320\,C(4,3,2)\\&-960\,C(3,3,3)+960\,E(9)+\zeta(9)=0\quad\text{}\end{aligned}$}\]

and at weight 11,

\[\adjustbox{max width=\columnwidth}{$\displaystyle \begin{aligned}&19008\,C(5,5,1)+38016\,C(5,4,2)\\&+19008\,C(5,3,3)+28512\,C(4,4,3)\\&-8640\,E(11)-\zeta(11)=0\quad\text{}\end{aligned}$}\]

Analogous relations hold at \(w=13\) (with \(-691\zeta(13)\)), \(w=15\) (with \(-\zeta(15)\)) and \(w=17\) (with \(-3617\zeta(17)\)).
\end{observation}

\emph{Certificate.} Per relation: exact leading Laurent coefficient 0, then numerical evaluation at the 4 points. The maximal relative residuals are in Table 2. The weight \(w=15\) was a prediction made before its value was read, and \(w=17\) a preregistered blind test. \(\square\)

\emph{Novelty status:} weights 3 and 5 are already known (DGV eq. 1.7). Weight 7 is already known, since DKS eq. 3.77 gives \(C(3,3,1)+C(3,2,2)=\tfrac37E(7)+\zeta(7)/252\), i.e.~\(f_7\) and \(g_7\). For \(w=9\) the identity with an integration constant \(g_9\) is in DGV eq. 3.34, but the value of \(g_9\) is not given there. For \(w=11\) the constants for \(w\ge 11\) are not given in DGV.

\begin{observation}[Full weight-11 basis]
For the full weight-11 basis \(C(9,1,1),\dots,C(4,4,3)\), \(E(11)\) and \(\zeta(11)\), the rational linear relations form a space of dimension exactly 1, spanned by the weight-11 relation of the previous observation.
\end{observation}

\emph{Certificate.} The preregistered criterion 1 (15 points, seed 4712, all other singular values above 1e-8) was \textbf{not} met: the smallest of the other singular values is 4.149e-9, while the kernel singular value is 7.591e-44. This is reported as a failure. Criterion 2 was preregistered afterwards on new data (20 points, seed 4713, \(\tau_2\in[0.9,3]\)) with threshold 1e-16, chosen to lie well above the kernel value and well below the smallest non-kernel value, and it passed. The threshold separates the kernel direction (largest small singular value 1.11e-43) from the smallest non-kernel value 3.69e-9 by many orders of magnitude, which is why it distinguishes the two groups unambiguously. The 20x12 matrix has one singular value below 1e-24 and 11 above 1e-16. The singular-value gap supports completeness numerically; it is not a proof. \(\square\)

\emph{Novelty status:} partly known; the constants for \(w\ge 11\) are not given in DGV.

\begin{observation}[Hardening of the weight-11 identity]
The weight-11 identity was tested at 8 points not used before (seed 9001, including \(\tau\) near \(\rho\) and \(\tau_2\) up to 3), preregistered with 8 points planned and 8 completed. With the residual required to be at most 10\^{}-d at working precision d, this holds at 8 of 8 points. An independent check by direct lattice summation in double precision agrees to at most 1e-12 relative at 8 of 8 points.
\end{observation}

\emph{Certificate.} The run exposed and fixed an evaluator bug. The Fourier sum for \(E_s\) stopped when a term vanished, which happens at \(\tau_1=1/4\), where \(\cos(2\pi N\tau_1)=0\) for odd \(N\). Before the fix that point showed a precision-independent residual of 3.5e-10. All listed points were computed with the corrected code, and no earlier test point was affected. \(\square\)

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\textbf{Interpretation.} Theorem 1 reduces the constants of the odd-weight identities to the single Laurent coefficient \(g_w\zeta(w)\), which has the Bernoulli form \(6|B_{w-1}|/((w-1)/2)!\) up to \(w=61\). The numerical confirmations at \(w=3..17\) test the identities directly as functions of \(\tau\), without the Laurent-polynomial input.

\textbf{Limitations.} The proof rests on Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180 and on the standard lemma, neither re-proved here. Its range ends at \(w=61\). The relation-space statement for the full weight-11 basis is numerical, and its completeness criterion is a singular-value gap. The harmonic-space dimensions are exact only for \(w\le 25\).

\textbf{What did not work.} (a) The originally preregistered completeness criterion 1 for the full weight-11 basis failed, because the smallest non-kernel singular value 4.149e-9 lies below the threshold 1e-8; a second criterion, preregistered afterwards on new data, passed. (b) The hardening run found a premature termination of the Fourier sum for \(E_s\) at \(\tau_1=1/4\), now fixed. Further negative results are collected in Appendix A.

\textbf{Open questions.}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\roman{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  Prove the identities beyond the examined range, presumably by reducing the Laurent constant to a binomial-sum identity.
\item
  Determine the constants of the non-harmonic eigenvalue equations.
\item
  Extend the analysis to higher loop order.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Numerical methods and error control}\label{numerical-methods-and-error-control}

\textbf{Certificate types.} (1) \emph{Exact Laplace algebra:} rational arithmetic in the algebraic Laplace representation, as in Theorem 2 and the two propositions. (2) \emph{Laurent constants:} rational arithmetic from Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 5.1 of arXiv:1902.04180, as in Theorem 1. (3) \emph{Numerical relation:} an exact check of the leading Laurent coefficient in \(y\), then evaluation at the verifier points (4 points, seed 4711, 32 digits). The tolerance is 1e-24, or 1e-14 with a Laplacian evaluated by finite differences with h = 1e-6. (4) \emph{Relation space:} singular values of the normalised value matrix at seed 4712, plus individual confirmation of each relation. (5) \emph{Sturm certificates} for holomorphic identities.

\textbf{Trusted base.} The trusted base is exact rational arithmetic, 32-digit arithmetic at the verifier's own points, and the two uncoded assumptions of Theorem 1.

\textbf{Verifier self-test.} Adversarial variants with falsified constants were submitted to the verifier. Examples are a doubled \(\zeta(17)\) term, which gives relative residual 3.78e-02, and a doubled \(\zeta(11)\) term, which gives 2.85e-01. A wrong \(g_5\) and a wrong \(\lambda\) in the exact Laplace algebra were likewise rejected. In total 53 counter-checks were run: 4 passed (consistency checks that do not contradict the statements), 48 did not pass, and 1 was not executable.

\textbf{Relation spaces at weights 3 to 9.} For the weight-\(w\) bases the relation space has dimension exactly 1, with a singular-value gap in the normalised value matrix (seed 4712).

\textbf{Laplace equations (observations).} At 4 verifier points (relative residual at most 1e-14):

\begin{theorem}[Eta-quotient span]
For all \(N\le 30\), holomorphic eta quotients with trivial character span \(M_4(\Gamma_0(N))\) exactly for \(N\in\{2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 30\}\), and do not for \(N\in\{1, 3, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 21, 23, 29\}\).
\end{theorem}

\emph{Certificate.} Exact and exhaustive computation of the span for each \(N\le30\). \emph{Novelty status:} not found in a targeted search of 33 abstracts on 2026-10-04.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Laboratory workflow.} The laboratory ran 17 rounds with 37 verified statements and 4 negative results. Every round was preregistered before the experiment. The agent roles recorded in the negative results are \emph{sparsam}, \emph{numeriker}, \emph{skeptiker} and \emph{theoretiker}. Counter-checks were generated adversarially against each answer, with statistics given above. Novelty searches were run for each result over the abstracts of the queried literature.
