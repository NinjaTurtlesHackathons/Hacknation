\section{Abstract}

We study indecomposable representations of quivers that are not of Dynkin type. We concentrate on star quivers (a centre with n leaves, no edges between leaves) and on 3- and 4-cycles, oriented and acyclic. A verifier-gated agentic lab checked the Tits-form type of each quiver with exact principal minors. It confirmed Gabriel's theorem for D4 and A3 by exhaustive counts over small finite fields. For the four-subspace problem it certified a one-parameter family of pairwise non-isomorphic bricks and the complete lists of indecomposables for the dimension vector δ over F_2, F_3 and F_5. For wild stars it certified the growth of the parameter count 1 − q(α) and Kac polynomials for the dimension vectors (2;1^n) at several n. For cycles it certified the Kac polynomial of δ independently of the orientation, and it checked the classification of indecomposables in the oriented case against exhaustive counts. All results are computer-verified and reproduce classical facts. No claim was refuted.

\section{1 Introduction}

\textbf{Contribution.} We collect exactly certified statements for stars and cycles and place them in the classical framework of Gabriel, Kac, Nazarova and Ringel {\scriptsize[C-lit1]}{\scriptsize[C-lit3]}{\scriptsize[C-lit4]}{\scriptsize[C-lit5]}{\scriptsize[C-lit6]}.

\section{2 Model}

Notation and definitions follow standard usage; only the roles they play in the cited claims matter here.

\begin{itemize}
\item A quiver is Q = (Q_0, Q_1, s, t). A representation assigns a vector space to each vertex and a linear map to each arrow, with morphisms, direct sums, indecomposables and dimension vectors in the usual sense. Krull–Schmidt holds.
\item The Tits form is q(x) = Σ x_i² − Σ_a x_{s(a)} x_{t(a)}.
\item Roots are real or imaginary. A real root has exactly one indecomposable and an imaginary root has infinitely many, with 1 − q(α) parameters {\scriptsize[C-lit3]}.
\item A_α(q) denotes the number of absolutely indecomposable representations over F_q. It is a monic polynomial with integer coefficients of degree 1 − q(α), independent of the orientation {\scriptsize[C-lit4]}.
\item Assumption: the lab's exact verifier works over Q, Q(t) and finite fields F_p. Absolute indecomposability is certified by dim End and the dimension of the radical J(End), and families are certified by elimination over Q(s,t) with logged pivots.
\end{itemize}

\section{3 Method: the verifier-gated agentic lab}

The lab ran 5 rounds and checked 76 statements, with 0 negative results; every round was preregistered before the experiment (prereg.md) {\scriptsize[C-methode]}. Each computed claim is paired with a red-team counter-check whose failure is recorded, for example "the type would be tame" or "the list is complete without its last element" {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-1-RT1]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-20-RT1]}. Exact certificates are principal minors, radical computation, Kac reduction, direct counting and Burnside counting over F_p, and elimination with logged pivots {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-19]}.

\section{4 Results}

Items are labelled Proposition (lettered A–P) when the evidence level is computed_rigorous. Literature claims are cited as such.

\subsubsection*{4.1 Gabriel's theorem and its confirmation}

Literature: a connected quiver has finitely many indecomposable isoclasses iff its graph is of Dynkin type A, D, E, and then indecomposables correspond bijectively to positive roots {\scriptsize[C-lit1]}. A proof by reflection (Coxeter) functors is due to Bernstein–Gelfand–Ponomarev {\scriptsize[C-lit2]}.

\textbf{Proposition A (computed_rigorous).} The Tits forms of star1, star2 and star3 are positive definite, by exact leading principal minors {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-1]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-2]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-3]}. star4 is positive semidefinite and not definite {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-4]}. star5, star6 and star7 are indefinite {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-5]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-6]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-7]}.

\textbf{Proposition B (computed_rigorous).} star3 has exactly 12 positive roots {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-11]}.

\textbf{Proposition C (computed_rigorous).} For star3 over F_2 and over F_3, and for all 0 < β ≤ [2, 1, 1, 1], there is exactly one indecomposable for real roots and none for non-roots {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-12]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-13]}. The same holds for A3 over F_2 with β ≤ [2, 2, 2] {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-14]}.

\subsubsection*{4.2 Beyond Dynkin}

Literature: for every quiver and algebraically closed field, the dimension vectors of indecomposables are exactly the positive roots; real roots have one indecomposable and imaginary roots have infinitely many {\scriptsize[C-lit3]}. A_α(q) is a polynomial {\scriptsize[C-lit4]}, with a counting formula {\scriptsize[C-lit7]} and non-negative coefficients {\scriptsize[C-lit8]}. The Euclidean quivers are exactly the tame ones, and their indecomposables are classified {\scriptsize[C-lit5]}. Their regular indecomposables fall into tubes parametrised by P^1, with finitely many exceptional tubes {\scriptsize[C-lit6]}.

\textbf{Proposition D (computed_rigorous).} The Kronecker quiver, cycle3_oriented and cycle4_oriented have positive semidefinite, non-definite Tits form {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-8]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-9]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-10]}.

\subsubsection*{4.3 Star quivers}

\textbf{Four-subspace problem (star4).}

\textbf{Proposition E (computed_rigorous).} The radical of the Tits form of star4 is generated by δ = [2, 1, 1, 1, 1] {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-15]}. δ is an imaginary root with 1 − q(α) = 1 {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-16]}, and so is [4, 2, 2, 2, 2] {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-17]}.

\emph{[entfernt: unbelegt — Zahl ohne Beleg]} The V_t are therefore pairwise non-isomorphic bricks, certified by elimination over Q(s, t) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-19]}.

\textbf{Proposition G (computed_rigorous).} The listed representations form a complete list of isoclasses of indecomposables with dimension vector [2, 1, 1, 1, 1] over F_2 (6 classes), F_3 (7 classes) and F_5 (9 classes) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-20]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-21]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-22]}. For q = 2, 3, 5, 7, A_δ(q) = 4 q^0 + 1 q^1, which is the Kac polynomial {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-18]}. A representation with two pairs of coinciding lines is decomposable over the algebraic closure {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-26]}.

\textbf{Proposition H (computed_rigorous).} Explicit absolutely indecomposable representations exist for the real roots [1, 1, 1, 1, 1], [3, 1, 1, 1, 1] and [2, 2, 1, 1, 1] {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-23]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-24]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-25]}. Over F_2 and F_3, for all β ≤ [3, 1, 1, 1, 1], real roots have exactly one indecomposable and non-roots have none {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-27]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-28]}.

\emph{Interpretation (uncertified).} The extra classes with exactly one coinciding pair of lines presumably sit in the exceptional tubes of the Euclidean case {\scriptsize[C-lit6]}. The lab did not certify this.

\textbf{Dimension vectors (2;1^n).}

\textbf{Proposition I (computed_rigorous).} The Kac polynomial for star4 is ((q+1)**3 − 1 − 7\emph{q)/(q}(q−1)) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-35]}. The Kac polynomials for star5 to star8 are

\begin{itemize}
\item star5: ((q+1)**4 − 1 − 15\emph{q)/(q}(q−1)) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-36]};
\item star6: ((q+1)**5 − 1 − 31\emph{q)/(q}(q−1)) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-37]};
\item star7: ((q+1)**6 − 1 − 63\emph{q)/(q}(q−1)) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-38]};
\item star8: ((q+1)**7 − 1 − 127\emph{q)/(q}(q−1)) {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-39]}.
\end{itemize}

Each is certified at deg + 2 primes, together with Kac's theorem that A_α is a polynomial of degree 1 − q(α) {\scriptsize[C-lit4]}.

\emph{Classification of (2;1^n).} The classification of the representations with dimension vector (2;1^n) as configurations of points in P^1, together with the closed formula in n, is derived in a companion ledger (results.tex). That derivation is not among the claims available here, so we do not state it as a theorem. The computer verification is Proposition I, and the closed formula in n is recorded only as an uncertified interpretation {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-35-I]}.

\section{5 Negative results and red-team findings}

No claim was refuted or contested, and there were 0 negative results {\scriptsize[C-methode]}. Every red-team counter-check failed as designed. Examples: the tame-type test for star1 {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R1-1-RT1]}, a second generator of the radical of star4 {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-15-RT1]}, and a shifted constant in the Kac polynomial {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-35-RT1]}. Further examples are an off-by-one polynomial for the 3-cycle {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R4-45-RT1]} and a decomposition of V_5 {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R2-19-RT1]}. \section{6 Limitations and open questions}

\begin{itemize}
\item The Kac polynomials are certified only at finitely many primes, together with Kac's theorem on polynomiality and degree {\scriptsize[C-lit4]}.
\item The results on stars and cycles reproduce classical facts {\scriptsize[C-lit1]}{\scriptsize[C-lit3]}{\scriptsize[C-lit5]}{\scriptsize[C-lit6]}. They are not new theorems.
\item Not computed: the counts for 2δ over F_5 for the 4-cycles. The scope of the exhaustive checks is bounded by the dimension vectors stated above. See also the verifier notes referenced in {\scriptsize[C-methode]}.
\item The interpretation of the extra classes as exceptional tubes, the closed formula in n, the general cycle formula A_δ(q) = q + n − 1 and A_{2δ} = A_δ remain uncertified {\scriptsize[C-quivers-R3-35-I]}{\scriptsize[C-quivers-R5-67-I]}.
\item Open: a uniform proof of the formulas from the certified instances; an extension to higher multiples of δ.
\end{itemize}
