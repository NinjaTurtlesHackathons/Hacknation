# Theory: Householder complexity of one-layer state tracking

Status legend: **[Lean]** machine-checked (expressivity/lean), **[hand]** proved by hand in this file, not machine-checked
(evidence level `hypothesis` in the paper until reviewed), **[cert]** every concrete instance re-verified by the exact verifier.

## Setting
G finite group, Sigma $\subseteq$ G \ {e} generating, word w = s_1 ... s_T, target g_t = s_t ... s_1. A *one-layer realisation* consists of
a state space V = R^{d x m}, maps A: Sigma -> R^{d x d}, B: Sigma -> V, an initial state h_0 and a readout phi: V -> G with
h_t = A(s_t) h_{t-1} + B(s_t) and phi(h_t) = g_t for all words and all t. It is *finite-state* if the set Q of reachable states is
finite, and it is *k-Householder* if rank(A(s) - I) <= k for every letter (this contains DeltaProduct with k factors
I - beta u u^T, beta $\in$ [0, 2], by L1).

Definitions. h(G, Sigma) = min over faithful real representations rho of G of max_s rank(rho(s) - I).
h*(G, Sigma) = min over (H, pi, (t_s), rho) of max_s rank(rho(t_s) - I), where H is a finite group, pi: H -> G a surjective
homomorphism, t_s $\in$ H with pi(t_s) = s generating H, and rho a faithful real representation of H. Clearly h* <= h (take H = G).

## L1 (rank lemma) [Lean]
If R_1, ..., R_k have rank <= 1 then rank(prod_i (I + R_i) - I) <= k. In particular a product of k generalized Householder
matrices differs from I by rank <= k. Lean: `rank_prod_sub_one_le`, `deltaproduct_rank_le`; tightness: `strengthened_bound_false`.

## L2 (compression lemma; necessity) [hand]
*Claim.* If a finite-state one-layer realisation of (G, Sigma) has rank(A(s) - I) <= k for all letters, then h*(G, Sigma) <= k.

*Proof.* For a word w let alpha_w be the affine map h -> A(w) h + B(w) obtained by composing the letter maps (A(w) = A(s_T)...A(s_1)).
Q is invariant under every alpha_s, so the alpha_w restricted to Q form a finite transformation monoid T, generated by the
restrictions of the alpha_s. Define psi: T -> G by psi(alpha_w|Q) = g(w). It is well defined: if alpha_w|Q = alpha_{w'}|Q then
g(w) = phi(alpha_w(h_0)) = phi(alpha_{w'}(h_0)) = g(w') (h_0 $\in$ Q). It is a monoid homomorphism onto G (concatenation; Sigma generates G).
Let K be the minimal two-sided ideal of the finite monoid T and e $\in$ K an idempotent (exists in every finite semigroup). Then
Gamma = eTe is a group with identity e (a maximal subgroup of the completely simple semigroup K). psi(e) is an idempotent of G,
hence psi(e) = 1, and psi(e t e) = psi(t), so psi restricted to Gamma is onto G.
Fix a word u with alpha_u|Q = e. Let Q_e = e(Q) = alpha_u(Q), W its affine span and U the direction space of W. e fixes Q_e
pointwise (e(e(q)) = e(q)), so alpha_u fixes W pointwise and A(u) acts as the identity on U.
For s $\in$ Sigma put x_s = e alpha_s e $\in$ Gamma; psi(x_s) = psi(alpha_s) = s, so the subgroup Gamma' generated by the x_s maps onto G.
Every element of Gamma maps Q_e bijectively to itself (Gamma is a group with identity e, and e is the identity on Q_e), hence its
affine extension alpha_{u v u} maps W to W; the restriction to W depends only on the element of Gamma (affine maps agreeing on the
affinely spanning set Q_e agree on W), so we get a homomorphism Gamma -> Aff(W). It is injective: an element acting trivially on W
acts trivially on Q_e, and an element t = ete of Gamma is determined by its action on Q_e (t(q) = t(e(q)) for all q $\in$ Q).
Taking linear parts gives a homomorphism lambda: Gamma -> GL(U). It is injective as well: if lambda(t) = I then t acts on W as a
translation; a translation of finite order is the identity.
Rank bound: the linear part of x_s on U is lambda(x_s) = A(u) A(s) A(u)|_U = A(u) A(s)|_U (A(u) = I on U), and for y $\in$ U,
lambda(x_s) y - y = A(u) A(s) y - A(u) y = A(u) (A(s) - I) y, so rank(lambda(x_s) - I_U) <= rank(A(s) - I) <= k.
Matrix-valued states: V = R^{d x m} with A acting from the left. Let C $\subseteq$ R^d be the span of all columns of all elements of U. C is
invariant under A(u) and under A(u)A(s) for elements of the group (columns of A H are A times columns of H); a word acts trivially on
U iff it acts trivially on C. Hence lambda'(t) = (linear part)|_C is a faithful representation of Gamma on C, and
rank(lambda'(x_s) - I_C) <= rank(A(u)(A(s) - I)) <= k. (For vector states m = 1 and C = U.)
Finally take H = Gamma', pi = psi|_{Gamma'}, t_s = x_s, rho = lambda' restricted to Gamma'; rho is faithful, pi(t_s) = s, the t_s
generate H, and rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= k. Hence h*(G, Sigma) <= k. QED.
*Remarks.* (i) No invertibility of A(s) is needed (beta = 1, projections, are allowed). (ii) The additive input term B(s) and the
readout are arbitrary. (iii) The finite-state assumption is essential: without a bound on the reachable set, infinite matrix groups
(free groups generated by two rotations) give other behaviour (analogist_candidates.md); finite precision makes every implemented
recurrence finite-state but not exactly linear.

## L3 (sufficiency) [hand, cert]
If (H, pi, (t_s), rho) has rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= k, a finite-state k-Householder realisation exists in dimension dim rho with
beta $\in$ {0, 2}: choose an H-invariant inner product (average any inner product over H) and an orthonormal basis for it, so rho is
orthogonal; by Cartan–Dieudonné (Scherk's form) each rho(t_s) is a product of exactly rank(rho(t_s) - I) reflections; pad with
identity factors (beta = 0). Take h_0 outside the finite union of the proper subspaces Fix(rho(x)), x $\neq$ 1 (rho faithful), so
x -> rho(x) h_0 is injective on H; the readout maps rho(x) h_0 to pi(x). Every concrete instance used in this work is re-verified
by `realisation.verify` (exact closure, consistent readout, invariant form, rank, explicit reflection factorisation).

**Theorem 1.** For every (G, Sigma): a finite-state one-layer k-Householder realisation exists iff k >= h*(G, Sigma). (L2 + L3.)

## L4 (order lemma) [hand]
If some letter s has order >= 3 then h*(G, Sigma) >= 2. *Proof.* Suppose rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= 1 with rho faithful on the finite
group H. If the rank is 0 then t_s = 1 and s = pi(1) = 1, impossible. Otherwise M = rho(t_s) = I + R with rank R = 1, R = v w^T,
R^2 = tau R with tau = w^T v, and M^n = I + c_n R with c_n = ((1 + tau)^n - 1)/tau (c_n = n if tau = 0). M has finite order N, so
c_N R = 0, hence c_N = 0: tau $\neq$ 0 and (1 + tau)^N = 1 with 1 + tau real, so 1 + tau = -1 (1 + tau = 1 means tau = 0). Then
M^2 = I + c_2 R = I + (((-1)^2 - 1)/(-2)) R = I, i.e. t_s^2 = 1 and s^2 = pi(t_s)^2 = 1, contradicting ord(s) >= 3. QED.

## L5 (diagonal families) [hand]
In L2 the compressed maps are A(u) A(s) restricted to an invariant subspace. If all A(s) are diagonal (real or complex), the
compressed maps commute, so Gamma' is abelian and G = pi(Gamma') is abelian. If the diagonal entries are real, each compressed map
is a restriction of a real diagonal matrix of finite order on its invariant subspace, so its eigenvalues are real roots of unity
(±1) and it is diagonalisable: Gamma' is an elementary abelian 2-group, hence so is G. If the entries lie in [0, 1], the only real
root of unity available is 1, so Gamma' is trivial and G = 1. Conversely an abelian G is realised by the count cover (one
coordinate per letter, complex rotation by 2 pi / ord(s)), and an elementary abelian 2-group by real ±1 coordinates.
*Statement:* one-layer finite-state realisations exist for diag_pos iff G = 1, for diag_pm iff G is an elementary abelian 2-group,
for cdiag iff G is abelian. (Consistent with Grazzi et al. 2024, Sarrof et al. 2024, Shakerinava et al. 2026; scout_evidence.md.)

## L6 (computability of h) [standard]
codim Fix rho(g) = dim rho - (1/|g|) sum_j chi_rho(g^j); codimensions add over direct sums; a sum of real irreducibles is faithful
iff the intersection of their kernels is trivial. Hence h(G, Sigma) = min over sets S of real irreducibles with trivial common
kernel of max_s sum_{psi $\in$ S} codim_psi(s): a finite optimisation over the real character table (chartab.py, GAP).

## L7 (abelian groups) [hand, cert]
For abelian G: h*(G, Sigma) = 1 if every letter is an involution, else 2. *Proof.* Lower bound: G $\neq$ 1 and L4. Upper bound (count
cover): H = prod_{s $\in$ Sigma} Z_{ord s}, t_s = generator of the s-th factor, pi(t_s) = s (a homomorphism because G is abelian),
rho = sum over letters of a reflection (ord s = 2) or a planar rotation by 2 pi / ord(s) (rank 2). Certified instances: Z3/all
(|H| = 9), Z2^3/all (|H| = 128).

## Corollaries (each instance certified by the verifier)
- **C1 (A5, the canonical NC1-complete word problem):** h*(A5, all) = 2 (icosahedral rotation group, dimension 3) and
  h*(A5, involutions) = 1 although h(A5, involutions) = 2: the full icosahedral reflection group H3 = A5 x Z2 (|H| = 120) maps onto A5
  with every involution lifted to a reflection (minus a rotation by pi). Plain DeltaNet (one reflection per token) tracks A5 exactly.
- **C2 (alphabet dependence):** h*(S5, transpositions) = 1 but h(S5, all) = 4 and h*(S5, all) $\in$ [2, 4]; the same group, different inputs.
- **C3 (non-monotonicity in circuit complexity):** the solvable groups Q8 and SL(2,3) have h = 4 (bounds [2, 4]), while the
  non-solvable A5 has h* = 2; Z2^k (in AC0[2]) has h = k but h* = 1.
- **C4 (representation matters):** h*(S4, all) = 2 (cube rotation group) whereas the permutation representation needs 3 (4-cycles).
