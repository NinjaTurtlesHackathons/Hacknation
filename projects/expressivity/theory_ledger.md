# Theory: Householder complexity of one-layer state tracking

Status legend: **[Lean]** machine-checked (expressivity/lean), **[hand]** proved by hand in this file, not machine-checked
(evidence level `hypothesis` in the paper until reviewed), **[cert]** every concrete instance re-verified by the exact verifier
(`results/certified.json`), **[literature]** taken from a cited source, not re-proved here. The hand proofs L2, L3, Theorem 1, L5
and L7 were reviewed by a red team (expressivity/analysis/redteam_theory.md: "valid with fix"); the fixes are incorporated below.

## Setting
G finite group, Sigma $\subseteq$ G \ {e} generating, word w = s_1 ... s_T (T >= 1), target g_t = s_t ... s_1. All spaces are **real**
(the field matters, see L2 remark (iv)). A *one-layer realisation* consists of a state space V = R^{d x m}, maps
A: Sigma -> R^{d x d} acting from the left, B: Sigma -> V, an initial state h_0 and a readout phi: V -> G with
h_t = A(s_t) h_{t-1} + B(s_t) and phi(h_t) = g_t for all words and all **t >= 1** (phi(h_0) is unconstrained). Transitions are
*token-local*: A and B depend on the current token only (no short convolution). Let
Q = {h_0} ∪ {h_t : all words, t >= 1}. The realisation is *finite-state* if Q is finite, and *k-Householder* if
rank(A(s) - I) <= k for every letter. A Householder factor is I - beta u u^T with ||u|| = 1 and beta in the **closed** interval
[0, 2]; by L1 a DeltaProduct transition with k such factors is k-Householder.

Definitions. h(G, Sigma) = min over faithful real representations rho of G of max_s rank(rho(s) - I).
h*(G, Sigma) = min over (H, pi, (t_s), rho) of max_s rank(rho(t_s) - I), where H is a finite group, pi: H -> G a homomorphism,
t_s $\in$ H with pi(t_s) = s, and rho a faithful real representation of H. Surjectivity of pi is automatic (pi(t_s) = s and Sigma
generates G). Requiring the t_s to generate H does not change h*: replace H by <t_s : s $\in$ Sigma>; rho stays faithful and pi stays
onto. Clearly h* <= h (take H = G). For G = 1 (Sigma empty) h* = h = 0; below G $\neq$ 1 unless said otherwise.
h is the alphabet-dependent, real version of Martino–Singh's "minimally generated in codimension k" (arXiv:1804.05089), which is
alphabet-free; codimension-<=2 generated orthogonal groups are classified by Lange–Mikhailova (arXiv:1509.06922). h* additionally
minimises over covering groups.

## L1 (rank lemma) [Lean]
If R_1, ..., R_k have rank <= 1 then rank(prod_i (I + R_i) - I) <= k. In particular a product of k generalized Householder
matrices differs from I by rank <= k (any beta, any u, any field). Lean: `rank_prod_sub_one_le`, `deltaproduct_rank_le`;
tightness: `strengthened_bound_false`.

## L2 (compression lemma; necessity) [hand]
*Claim.* If a real finite-state one-layer realisation of (G, Sigma) has rank(A(s) - I) <= k for all letters, then
h*(G, Sigma) <= k.

*Proof.* For a nonempty word w let alpha_w be the affine map h -> A(w) h + B(w) obtained by composing the letter maps
(A(w) = A(s_T)...A(s_1)). Q is invariant under every alpha_s, so the restrictions alpha_w|Q (w nonempty) form a finite
transformation **semigroup** T, generated by the restrictions of the alpha_s. Define psi: T -> G by psi(alpha_w|Q) = g(w). It is
well defined: if alpha_w|Q = alpha_{w'}|Q then g(w) = phi(alpha_w(h_0)) = phi(alpha_{w'}(h_0)) = g(w') (h_0 $\in$ Q, and the readout
is constrained because w, w' are nonempty). It is a semigroup homomorphism (concatenation). It is onto G: the image is a
subsemigroup of the **finite** group G containing Sigma, and in a finite group every subsemigroup is a subgroup
(s^{-1} = s^{ord(s)-1}), so the image is <Sigma> = G.
(Equivalently one may use the monoid including the empty word: if alpha_w|Q = id for a nonempty w, then for every nonempty v,
"w then v" has the same restriction as v, so g(v) g(w) = g(v) and g(w) = e; hence psi(id) = e is consistent.)
Let K be the minimal two-sided ideal of the finite semigroup T and e $\in$ K an idempotent (exists in every finite semigroup). Then
Gamma = eTe is a group with identity e (a maximal subgroup of the completely simple semigroup K). psi(e) is an idempotent of G,
hence psi(e) = 1, and psi(e t e) = psi(t), so psi restricted to Gamma is onto G.
Fix a nonempty word u with alpha_u|Q = e. Let Q_e = e(Q) = alpha_u(Q), W its affine span and U the direction space of W. e fixes
Q_e pointwise (e(e(q)) = e(q)), so alpha_u fixes W pointwise and A(u) acts as the identity on U.
For s $\in$ Sigma put x_s = e alpha_s e $\in$ Gamma; psi(x_s) = psi(alpha_s) = s, so the subgroup Gamma' generated by the x_s maps onto G.
Every element of Gamma maps Q_e bijectively to itself (Gamma is a group with identity e, and e is the identity on Q_e), hence its
affine extension alpha_{u v u} maps W to W; the restriction to W depends only on the element of Gamma (affine maps agreeing on the
affinely spanning set Q_e agree on W), so we get a homomorphism Gamma -> Aff(W). It is injective: an element acting trivially on W
acts trivially on Q_e, and an element t = ete of Gamma is determined by its action on Q_e (t(q) = t(e(q)) for all q $\in$ Q).
Taking linear parts gives a homomorphism lambda: Gamma -> GL(U). It is injective as well: if lambda(t) = I then t acts on W as a
translation; a translation of finite order is the identity (characteristic 0).
Rank bound: the linear part of x_s on U is lambda(x_s) = A(u) A(s) A(u)|_U = A(u) A(s)|_U (A(u) = I on U), and for y $\in$ U,
lambda(x_s) y - y = A(u) A(s) y - A(u) y = A(u) (A(s) - I) y, so rank(lambda(x_s) - I_U) <= rank(A(s) - I) <= k.
Matrix-valued states: V = R^{d x m} with A acting from the left. Let C $\subseteq$ R^d be the span of all columns of all elements of U. C is
invariant under A(u) and under A(u)A(s) for elements of the group (columns of A H are A times columns of H); a word acts trivially on
U iff it acts trivially on C. Hence lambda'(t) = (linear part)|_C is a faithful representation of Gamma on C, and
rank(lambda'(x_s) - I_C) <= rank(A(u)(A(s) - I)) <= k. (For vector states m = 1 and C = U. The reduction to C is needed: on U the
operator I_m ⊗ (A(s) - I) can have rank up to k m.) Since Gamma maps onto G $\neq$ 1, Gamma $\neq$ 1 and dim C >= 1.
Finally take H = Gamma', pi = psi|_{Gamma'}, t_s = x_s, rho = lambda' restricted to Gamma'; rho is faithful, pi(t_s) = s, the t_s
generate H, and rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= k. Hence h*(G, Sigma) <= k. QED.
*Remarks.* (i) No invertibility of A(s) is needed (beta = 1, projections, are allowed). (ii) The additive input term B(s) is
arbitrary. (iii) The finite-state assumption is essential: without a bound on the reachable set, infinite matrix groups
(free groups generated by two rotations) give other behaviour (analogist_candidates.md); finite precision makes every implemented
recurrence finite-state but not exactly linear. (iv) Field: the proof holds verbatim over C with complex rank, giving the
analogous complex invariant. It does **not** bound the real h* by the complex rank: Z3 is realised by the 1x1 complex rotation
e^{2 pi i / 3}, whose complex rank(A - I) is 1, while h*(Z3, all) = 2 (red-team script t13). Realification turns a complex
realisation with complex rank <= k into a real one with rank <= 2k, so it only gives h* <= 2k. (v) The readout may also depend on
the current token, phi(h_t, s_t) (as in models that read out S_t q_t with q_t depending on x_t): if alpha_w|Q = alpha_{w'}|Q,
then for every nonempty v the words "w then v" and "w' then v" reach the same state with the same last token, so
g(v) g(w) = g(v) g(w') and g(w) = g(w') by cancellation; psi stays well defined and the proof is unchanged.

## L3 (sufficiency) [hand, cert]
If (H, pi, (t_s), rho) has rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= k, a finite-state k-Householder realisation exists in dimension dim rho with
beta $\in$ {0, 2} (beta = 2 **exactly** and unit keys for the reflections): choose an H-invariant inner product (average any inner
product over H) and an orthonormal basis for it, so rho is orthogonal; by Cartan–Dieudonné (Scherk's form) each rho(t_s) is a
product of exactly rank(rho(t_s) - I) reflections; pad with identity factors (beta = 0). Take B = 0 and h_0 outside the finite
union of the proper subspaces Fix(rho(x)), x $\neq$ 1 (rho faithful), so x -> rho(x) h_0 is injective on H; the readout maps
rho(x) h_0 to pi(x). Generation of H by the t_s is not used.
*Zero initial state.* If the architecture fixes h_0 = 0 (DeltaNet convention S_0 = 0, right action
S <- S (I - beta k k^T) + beta v k^T), the construction above with B = 0 would be constant. Token-local fix: pick a generic row c
and set the value of every factor to v_j = -c k_j. Then each factor maps S + c to (S + c)(I - beta k k^T), so S_t + c = c M_t
with M_t the product of the realised reflections, which determines x_t (generic c). Red-team script t14 verified this exactly
for S5/transpositions with one reflection per token (120 reachable states, consistent readout).
Every concrete instance used in this work is re-verified by `realisation.verify` (exact closure, consistent readout, invariant
form, rank, explicit reflection factorisation).

**Theorem 1.** Let G be finite and Sigma $\subseteq$ G \ {e} generating. A **real**, **finite-state**, **one-layer** realisation with
**token-local** transitions and arbitrary readout phi(h_t) (or phi(h_t, s_t)) satisfying rank(A(s) - I) <= k for every letter
exists iff k >= h*(G, Sigma). Equivalently (L1 + L3), with transitions that are products of k Householder factors with
beta $\in$ [0, 2] (beta = 2 must be allowed). (L2 gives "only if", L3 gives "if".)
*Remarks.* Requiring generating lifts in the definition of h* does not change h* (see Definitions; necessity even produces
generating lifts, sufficiency does not need them), and surjectivity of pi is redundant. No counterexample was found by the red
team (exhaustive small-grid search for a rank-1 affine realisation of Z3; singular, affine and matrix-state stress tests).

**Corollary 1a (open beta range) [hand].** If every Householder factor has unit key and beta $\in$ [0, 2) (for example
beta = 2 sigmoid(z)), then no nontrivial group is exactly realisable by a finite-state one-layer realisation, for any k.
*Proof.* Each factor F = I - beta k k^T has ||F|| <= 1, and ||F y|| = ||y|| iff F y = y (beta $\in$ (0, 2); beta = 0 gives F = I).
In L2 the compressed map M = A(u) A(s) restricted to C has finite order N, so ||y|| = ||M^N y|| <= ||M y|| <= ||y|| for y $\in$ C.
Hence every factor along the chain fixes its input, M|_C = I and x_s = e, so s = psi(x_s) = e, a contradiction. QED.
Consequently, trained models with open parameterisations (sigmoid beta, tanh or sigmoid gates) can only **approximate** the
exact realisations of Theorem 1; claims about them are claims about approximation in the closed-family limit. The analogous
statement for the diagonal families is in L5.

## L4 (order lemma) [Lean]
If some letter s has order >= 3 then h*(G, Sigma) >= 2. Lean: `sq_eq_one_of_rank_le_one_of_pow_eq_one` (a real matrix of finite
order with rank(M - I) <= 1 is an involution) and `no_single_householder_lift` (for a faithful real representation rho of a finite
group H, any homomorphism pi and pi(t) = s with ord(s) >= 3, rank(rho(t) - I) > 1). The step to h* also uses that rank 0 is
impossible (t_s = 1 gives s = pi(1) = e). The matrix statement is classical: over subfields of R a reflection (finite order,
fixing a hyperplane pointwise) has order 2 (Geck–Malle, arXiv:math/0311012) [literature].
*Proof sketch* (as formalised). M = I + R with rank R = 1, R = v w^T, R^2 = tau R with tau = w^T v, and M^n = I + c_n R with
c_n = ((1 + tau)^n - 1)/tau (c_n = n if tau = 0). Finite order N gives c_N = 0, hence tau $\neq$ 0 and (1 + tau)^N = 1 with 1 + tau
real, so 1 + tau = -1 and M^2 = I. Then t_s^2 = 1 (rho faithful) and s^2 = 1, contradicting ord(s) >= 3.

## L5 (diagonal families) [hand]
Families: diag_pos (real diagonal entries in [0, 1]), diag_pm (real entries in [-1, 1]), cdiag (complex entries with
|lambda| <= 1). All parameter sets are **closed**. "Diagonal" means diagonal on vec(h) (Mamba-style elementwise decay); this is
covered by flattening the state to a vector (m = 1), not by the left-action form.
In L2 the compressed maps are A(u) A(s) restricted to the invariant subspace C. If all A(s) are diagonal, the compressed maps
commute, so Gamma' is abelian and G = pi(Gamma') is abelian. For cdiag this uses L2 over C (remark (iv)); the Setting is real.
If the diagonal entries are real, each compressed map is the restriction of a real diagonal matrix to an invariant subspace,
and this **restriction** has finite order (the entries of A(s) themselves are unrestricted); the restriction is diagonalisable
with eigenvalues among the diagonal entries, so finite order forces eigenvalues ±1: Gamma' is generated by commuting involutions,
an elementary abelian 2-group, hence so is G. If the entries lie in [0, 1], the only root of unity available is 1, so the
restriction is the identity, Gamma' is trivial and G = 1. Conversely an abelian G is realised by the count cover (one coordinate
per letter, complex rotation by 2 pi / ord(s), |lambda| = 1), and an elementary abelian 2-group by real ±1 coordinates.
*Statement:* one-layer finite-state realisations exist for diag_pos only for G = 1 (no nontrivial group is realisable), for
diag_pm iff G is an elementary abelian 2-group, for cdiag (|lambda| <= 1) iff G is abelian. (Consistent with Grazzi et al. 2024,
arXiv:2411.12537; Sarrof et al. 2024, arXiv:2405.17394; Shakerinava et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.01959; scout_evidence.md.)
*Open parameter sets.* For the open sets used in training (sigmoid $\in$ (0, 1), tanh $\in$ (-1, 1), |lambda| < 1) the compressed map on
C is a strict contraction and cannot have finite order unless C = 0; since dim C >= 1 for G $\neq$ 1, no nontrivial group is exactly
realisable. Predictions for trained diagonal models (e.g. diag_pm on Z2^3) concern approximation in the closed-family limit.

## L6 (computability of h) [standard]
codim Fix rho(g) = dim rho - (1/|g|) sum_j chi_rho(g^j); codimensions add over direct sums; a sum of real irreducibles is faithful
iff the intersection of their kernels is trivial. Hence h(G, Sigma) = min over sets S of real irreducibles with trivial common
kernel of max_s sum_{psi $\in$ S} codim_psi(s): a finite optimisation over the real character table (chartab.py, GAP).

## L7 (abelian groups) [hand, cert]
For abelian G $\neq$ 1: h*(G, Sigma) = 1 if every letter is an involution, else 2. *Proof.* Lower bound: G $\neq$ 1 gives h* >= 1, and
L4 gives h* >= 2 if some letter has order >= 3. Upper bound (count cover): H = prod_{s $\in$ Sigma} Z_{ord s}, t_s = generator of the
s-th factor, pi(t_s) = s (a homomorphism because the images commute and s^{ord s} = e), rho = sum over letters of a reflection
(ord s = 2) or a planar rotation by 2 pi / ord(s) (rank 2); the direct sum of faithful blocks is faithful on the product. The count
cover has dimension #involution letters + 2 #other letters; this is an upper bound, **not** the minimal dimension in general
(Z3/all: the cover uses dimension 4, a single planar rotation uses 2). Certified instances: Z3/all (h* = 2, certified witness the
permutation representation, |H| = 3, dimension 3), Z2^2/all (count cover, |H| = 8), Z2^3/all (count cover, |H| = 128).
(For G = 1, Sigma = ∅ the statement as written would give 1, but h* = 0; hence G $\neq$ 1.)

## Relation to Complex KDA (arXiv:2609.24797)
Siems et al. 2026 (Complex KDA, arXiv:2609.24797; full comparison in ckda_comparison.md) prove, for one layer with any number of
independent heads, arbitrary additive terms and arbitrary many-to-one decoders, under finite reachability of exact states, that
S5 with all inputs cannot be tracked if every head transition is non-expansive (||A||_2 <= 1) and has at most one non-real
unit-circle eigenvalue pair (their Theorem 4 = Theorem 22); for (gated) DeltaProduct with beta_j $\in$ [0, 2] four Householders are
therefore the minimum for S5 (their App. C.6).
*Compression: theirs vs ours.* Both arguments pass from a finite-state tracker to a finite group mapping onto G via an idempotent
word. Their reduction (Props. 20–21) picks a word whose linear part has **minimal Frobenius norm** and needs **non-expansion** to
force the transitions to act orthogonally on its image; the compressed maps are genuine restrictions of A_g, so the **spectrum**
(eigenvalues with multiplicities) transfers, and several heads are handled. Our L2 uses the **minimal two-sided ideal** of the
finite transition semigroup, needs **no norm assumption** (expansive, singular, non-unit keys, beta outside [0, 2] allowed), and
produces compressions A(u)A(s)|_C rather than restrictions, so only the **rank bound** rank(· - I) <= k transfers. Neither result
contains the other: theirs covers gated and multi-head transitions (where rank(A - I) is not small), ours yields the exact
characterisation of Theorem 1 for every finite group and alphabet.

## C5 (S5 with the full alphabet) [hand + literature]
**h*(S5, all) = 4**, under exact arithmetic with finitely many reachable exact states (our finite-state setting), for an alphabet
containing a 5-cycle and a transposition (in particular Sigma = S5 \ {e}).
*Upper bound [cert]:* the permutation representation (dimension 5), where a 5-cycle has rank(rho(s) - I) = 4 (certified row
S5/all, upper bound 4).
*Lower bound.* Suppose h*(S5, all) <= 3 via (H, pi, (t_s), rho).
1. [hand, ours] Average an inner product over H and change basis, so rho is orthogonal; rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= 3 is unchanged by
   similarity.
2. [hand, ours] An orthogonal matrix is diagonalisable over C, so rank(rho(t_s) - I) <= 3 means at most 3 eigenvalues differ from
   1, hence at most one non-real conjugate pair (this matches their counting with gate alpha = 1).
3. [hand, ours] Build the tracker of L3 / their Lemma 11: matrix state S_0 = I, A_s = rho(t_s), A_e = I, B = 0, decoder
   S -> pi(rho^{-1}(S)). One head, |H| reachable states, exact updates, ||A_s||_2 = 1, inputs all of S5.
4. [literature] Their Theorem 4 / Theorem 22 says no such tracker exists. Contradiction, so h*(S5, all) >= 4.
Combined with L2 (Theorem 1), this extends their S5 lower bound to the norm-free rank setting: no real finite-state one-layer
realisation with rank(A(s) - I) <= 3 for all letters, expansive or not, tracks S5. This extension is ours, not in their paper. For
literal DeltaProduct with unit keys and beta $\in$ [0, 2] the transitions are already non-expansive and their theorem applies
directly. Our L2 does not cover gated transitions; for those cite their result alone.
*Smaller alphabets* [hand, ours, unreviewed]: their proof uses only the letters c = (12345) and t = (12). The comparison report
observes (not their claim) that the argument therefore works for any alphabet containing a 5-cycle and a transposition; both
conjugacy types of the pair (adjacent and non-adjacent transposition) give a commutator of order 3. This would give
h*(S5, tn) = 4 for the generating pair {transposition, 5-cycle} (certified interval [2, 4]).

## Corollaries (instances certified by the verifier unless marked)
- **C1 (A5, the canonical NC1-complete word problem):** h*(A5, all) = 2 (icosahedral rotation group, dimension 3) and
  h*(A5, involutions) = 1 although h(A5, involutions) = 2: the full icosahedral reflection group H3 = A5 x Z2 (|H| = 120) maps onto A5
  with every involution lifted to a reflection (minus a rotation by pi). Plain DeltaNet (one reflection per token) tracks A5 exactly.
- **C2 (alphabet dependence):** h*(S5, transpositions) = 1 [cert] but h(S5, all) = 4 and h*(S5, all) = 4 (C5: upper bound [cert],
  lower bound [hand + literature], under exact finite reachability); the same group, different inputs.
- **C3 (non-monotonicity in circuit complexity):** the solvable groups Q8 and SL(2,3) have h = 4 (L6 character-table computation;
  for Q8 the library certificate gives 6 (permutation witness of dimension 8) and the explorer's cover certificate gives 3, see below; SL(2,3) is in the atlas, not in
  certified.json), so h* $\in$ [2, 4] for both, while the non-solvable A5 has h* = 2 [cert]; Z2^k (in AC0[2]) has h = k but h* = 1
  (Z2^3 [cert]).
- **C4 (representation matters):** h*(S4, all) = 2 (cube rotation group) whereas the permutation representation needs 3 (4-cycles).

## Open
h*(SL(2,3), all) (gap between the lower bound 2 and the upper bound h = 4); h*(Q8, all) = 3 rests on a hand lower bound (certified upper bound 3);
h*(S5, tn) = 4 pending review of the smaller-alphabet step in C5 (certified interval [2, 4]). h*(S5, all) is no longer open under
the conditions of C5 (exact finite reachability, alphabet containing a 5-cycle and a transposition). Also open: finite datatypes
with casting instead of finite exact reachability (open in arXiv:2609.24797 as well); multi-layer trade-offs between depth and
Householders; the effect of the short convolution; learnability.
